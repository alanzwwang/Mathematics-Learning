📌 Keep the big ideas in sight

Real and imaginary are separate piles.
i² = −1 — the minus sign is the whole point.
Multiplying spins and stretches; it never just "gets bigger."

Students mix up these five ideas every single year. Learn them now, and they won't catch you!

Mistake 1: "i is just a mystery letter, like x"

i works like any variable?   ❌

x can stand for anything — that's its job. i is ONE specific number with ONE special rule: i² = −1. Treat it like a number that carries a tiny superpower, not like a placeholder. When you see i², something always happens: it becomes −1. ✅

Mistake 2: "i² = 1, because squaring kills the minus"

i squared... signs cancel, so 1?   ❌

i² = −1 BY DEFINITION — it's the one rule, the reason i exists. The flip is the whole point. (And a sneaky bonus trap: (−i)² = (−1)² · i² = −1 too — BOTH i and −i square to −1!) ✅

Mistake 3: "3 + 2i simplifies to 5"

Merge the piles: 3 + 2i = 5? or 5i?   ❌

Apples plus oranges isn't 5 of anything. Real and imaginary are separate piles forever — 3 + 2i is already fully simplified. You can only combine real with real and imaginary with imaginary, during an addition. ✅

Mistake 4: "|3 + 4i| = 3 + 4 = 7"

Length = add the parts?   ❌

Length is the DIAGONAL of the right triangle, and diagonals come from Pythagoras, not from adding: |3 + 4i| = √(9 + 16) = 5. (Adding the parts measures a staircase, not the arrow.) ✅

Mistake 5: "Imaginary means fake"

These numbers are just pretend?   ❌

The name was literally an insult — the philosopher Descartes coined "imaginary" to mock them, and it stuck. But the electric grid powering your home, the wings of the plane overhead, the Wi-Fi in your room, and the quantum rules inside every atom are all computed with i. Imaginary is a name, not a verdict. ✅
