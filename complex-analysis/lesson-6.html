🌟 Length and Direction — The Spin-and-Stretch Secret

📖 Lesson 6 of 9

👋 Start123456789🏋️ Practice✅ Answers

📌 Key idea of this section

Every complex number is a LENGTH and a DIRECTION.
When you multiply: lengths multiply, angles ADD.

How far from home?

Every arrow has a length — its distance from 0. Mathematicians write it with bars: |3 + 4i| means "the length of 3 + 4i."

And here's where a 2,500-year-old friend appears. The arrow to (3, 4) is the diagonal of a 3-by-4 right triangle, so Pythagoras does the measuring:

|3 + 4i| = √(3² + 4²) = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5

(There it is — the third line from the welcome page!) The length of a + bi is √(a² + b²), and it has a fancy name: the modulus. Some lengths come out whole — |6 + 8i| = 10, |4 + 3i| = 5 — and some don't: |1 + i| = √2, which is fine; √2 just means "the number that squares to 2."

Every arrow also has a direction — the angle it makes with the real axis. East is 0°, i points north at 90°, −1 points west at 180°, and 1 + i splits the difference at 45° — halfway between east and north, since its two parts are equal.

The secret

Now the punchline of the whole lesson. Watch lengths and angles when numbers multiply. Take (1 + i) × (1 + i) = 2i, from Lesson 5:

lengths:  |1 + i| × |1 + i| = √2 × √2 = 2 = |2i| ✅
angles:   45° + 45° = 90° — and 2i really does point north ✅

It works for i × i = −1 too: lengths 1 × 1 = 1 ✅, angles 90° + 90° = 180° ✅. Multiplying complex numbers MULTIPLIES the lengths and ADDS the angles. Spin and stretch — that's all multiplication ever was!

This is why squaring 1 + i landed so neatly: squaring doubles the angle (45° → 90°) and squares the length (√2 → 2). Cubing would triple the angle. Grown-ups call this De Moivre's idea, and it turns impossible-looking power problems into simple angle arithmetic.

And here's the honest name-drop: when multiplication is this smooth — every product a graceful spin-and-stretch — the whole calculus of these numbers becomes smooth too. THAT deep, smooth, spinning subject is complex analysis. You're standing in its front yard.
