🛠️ The Mirror Trick — Conjugates and Division

📖 Lesson 7 of 9

👋 Start123456789🏋️ Practice✅ Answers

📌 Key idea of this section

The conjugate flips the imaginary sign: a + bi ↔ a − bi.
(a + bi)(a − bi) = a² + b² — always real!

The mirror twin

Every complex number has a mirror twin across the real axis: the conjugate of 2 + 3i is 2 − 3i. Same real part, flipped imaginary part — the arrow reflects from north-of-the-line to south-of-it.

Now multiply the twins and watch the miracle:

(2 + 3i)(2 − 3i) = 4 − 6i + 6i − 9i²
                 = 4 + 9
                 = 13 = 2² + 3²

The middle rooms cancel (−6i and +6i), and the trapdoor flips the last sign (+9 instead of −9). Always:

(a + bi)(a − bi) = a² + b²

Notice: a² + b² is exactly the number under the √ in the length formula. So twins multiply to the SQUARED length — a pure, positive, real number. The imaginary parts destroy each other.

Dividing: keep, change... mirror!

What could (1 + 3i) ÷ (1 + i) possibly mean? The same thing it always means: WHAT NUMBER times (1 + i) gives (1 + 3i)?

The trick is a cousin of keep-change-flip: multiply top and bottom by the conjugate of the bottom. The bottom turns real, and real division is easy:

1 + 3i       (1 + 3i)(1 − i)     4 + 2i
──────   =   ───────────────   =  ─────  =  2 + i
1 + i        (1 + i)(1 − i)        2

Top: (1 + 3i)(1 − i) = 1 − i + 3i − 3i² = 4 + 2i. Bottom: (1 + i)(1 − i) = 1 + 1 = 2.

Check it backwards: does (2 + i)(1 + i) = 1 + 3i? 2 + 2i + i + i² = 1 + 3i ✅. Division works!

So the full toolbox is complete: add, subtract, multiply, divide. The complex numbers are a whole self-contained number world — nothing you compute ever kicks you out of it.
