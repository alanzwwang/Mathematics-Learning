  • 87.
    (a) −1 + 2i. (b) −2 − i. (c) 2 + i — back home! Four quarter
    turns make a full turn. (d) 9 minutes = 2 full loops + 1 minute,
    so it matches the 1-minute answer: −1 + 2i.
  • 88.
    (a) 5 (a 3-4-5 triangle). (b) 5 — mirroring doesn't change
    distance. (c) (3 + 4i) + (3 − 4i) = 6. (d) The imaginary parts
    are exact opposites (+bi and −bi), so they always cancel; any
    number plus its conjugate is twice its real part — pure real.
  • 89.
    False. (−i)² = (−1)² · i² = 1 · (−1) = −1. BOTH i and −i square
    to −1 — that's why x² = −1 has two solutions.
  • 90.
    Two quarter turns make a half turn, and a half turn is exactly
    what ×(−1) does — i² = −1 said in spinning language. Watch:
    (2 + i) · i · i = (2 + i) · (−1) = −2 − i, which is −(2 + i).
    Same destination, every time.

Challenge

  • 91.
    (a) 1 + 2i + i² = 2i. (b) (1 + i)(2i) = 2i + 2i² = −2 + 2i.
    (c) (2i)² = 4i² = −4. (d) Lengths: √2, 2, 2√2, 4 — each step
    multiplies the length by √2. Angles: 45°, 90°, 135°, 180° —
    each step adds 45°. The powers spiral outward!
    (e) Eight steps: angle 8 × 45° = 360° (pointing east — a real
    number!) and length (√2)⁸ = 2⁴ = 16, so (1 + i)⁸ = 16.
    Check: (1 + i)⁸ = ((1 + i)⁴)² = (−4)² = 16 ✅.
  • 92.
    Sample: start at 3 + i (a dancer facing east). ×i → −1 + 3i
    (facing north). ×i → −3 − i (west). ×i → 1 − 3i (south). A
    fourth ×i brings her back to 3 + i — home! Whatever point you
    picked, four quarter turns always bring you back.
  • 93.
    (a) (3i)² = 9i² = −9 ✅ and (−3i)² = 9i² = −9 ✅ — two answers:
    3i and −3i. (b) 5i and −5i. (c) Two: i and −i. (Grown-up
    promise: with complex numbers, EVERY quadratic has exactly two
    roots — that's the payoff for inventing them.)
  • 94.
    (a) i · (2 + i) = −1 + 2i — but the landing spot was 1 + 3i,
    so w is not i: it's not a pure quarter turn. (b) (2 + i)(1 + i) =
    2 + 2i + i + i² = 1 + 3i ✅ — w = 1 + i works. (It's a quarter
    turn AND a stretch by √2, since |1 + i| = √2!) (c) (3 − i)(1 + i)
    = 4 + 2i. (d) w = 1 + i again — (1 + i)² = 2i. Same mystery
    multiplier!
  • 95.
    |1 + i| = √2 and |2 − i| = √5; their product is √10. The numbers
    multiply to (1 + i)(2 − i) = 2 − i + 2i − i² = 3 + i, and
    |3 + i| = √(9 + 1) = √10 ✅ — the lengths multiply! Your own
    pair should agree too: |z · w| = |z| · |w| for every pair.
    (Lesson 6 explains why: multiplying multiplies lengths.)
  • 96.
    (a) z + w = 4 + i, whose conjugate is 4 − i. Separately:
    conj(z) + conj(w) = (1 − 2i) + (3 + i) = 4 − i. SAME ✅.
    (b) z · w = 3 − i + 6i − 2i² = 5 + 5i, whose conjugate is
    5 − 5i. Separately: (1 − 2i)(3 + i) = 3 + i − 6i − 2i² =
    5 − 5i. SAME ✅. (c) The mirror of a sum is the sum of the
    mirrors — and the mirror of a product is the product of the
    mirrors. Conjugates play nicely with everything!
  • 97.
    (a) (1 + i)(1 + i) ÷ ((1 − i)(1 + i)) = 2i ÷ 2 = i.
    (b) Both have length √2, so the stretch factor is 1 — pure spin.
    From −45° up to +45° is exactly 90°: a quarter turn, landing on
    the imaginary axis. (c) i · (1 − i) = i − i² = i + 1 = 1 + i ✅.
  • 98.
    (a) 1 + i + (−1) + (−i) = 0 — the four spokes cancel in pairs!
    (b) i⁴ · (1 + i + i² + i³) = 1 · 0 = 0. (c) One hundred terms =
    25 complete blocks of 4 → 25 × 0 = 0. (d) 102 terms = 25 blocks
    plus the first 2 terms of the next cycle → 1 + i.
  • 99.
    (a) x² = (1 + 2i)² = 1 + 4i + 4i² = −3 + 4i. Then −2x =
    −2 − 4i. Total: (−3 + 4i) + (−2 − 4i) + 5 = 0 ✅.
    (b) (1 − 2i)² = −3 − 4i; −2x = −2 + 4i; total
    (−3 − 4i) + (−2 + 4i) + 5 = 0 ✅. (c) The imaginary parts were
    exact opposites and cancelled — conjugates strike again! Complex
    roots of real equations always arrive in mirror-twin pairs, so
    the equation itself stays real.
  • 100.
    Sample sum problem: "A wizard walks 2 + 3i, then 4 + i. Where
    is she?" → 6 + 4i. Sample spin problem: "Treasure sits at
    5 + 2i. The map says multiply by i. Where is it?" → −2 + 5i.
    Bonus length-5: "How far is 4 + 3i from zero?" → 5. Your
    problems can be completely different — if one answer is a slide
    (parts added) and the other is a quarter turn (×i), you're right!
