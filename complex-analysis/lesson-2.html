🧩 The Complex Map — Right for Real, Up for Imaginary

📖 Lesson 2 of 9

👋 Start123456789🏋️ Practice🏆 Quiz

📌 Key idea of this section

a + bi lives at the point (a, b):
a steps right, then b steps up.

One line is too small

Where does i go on the number line? Trick question: NOWHERE. Every spot on the line is already taken by a real number, and i isn't one. That's not a problem — it's a hint. If i won't fit on the line, give it a whole MAP.

Here's how. A complex number has two parts, and you already know something else with two parts: coordinates! So let 3 + 2i live at the point (3, 2) — go right 3 for the real part, up 2 for the imaginary part:

          imaginary
              ▲
          2 ─ ┤         • 3 + 2i
              │
          1 ─ • i
              │
──────•───────┼─────────•──────► real
      −2      │         3
              │
         −1 ─ ┤
              │
         −2 ─ • −2i

Look around the map:

  • ·
    The flat horizontal line is the REAL axis — every ordinary number lives there: 4 sits at (4, 0), −2 sits at (−2, 0).
  • ·
    The tall vertical line is the IMAGINARY axis — the pure imaginary numbers live there: i at (0, 1), −2i at (0, −2).
  • ·
    Everything off both lines — like 3 + 2i — is a full-blown complex number, with a foot in both worlds.
  • ·
    Zero sits at the crossing, the center of everything.

Arrows from home

One more way to see a complex number — and it's the way that matters most later. Draw an ARROW from 0 out to the point. Then 3 + 2i isn't just a dot; it's a journey: 3 east, 2 north. Every complex number is an arrow with a length and a direction. Keep that picture. Lesson 6 blows it wide open.
