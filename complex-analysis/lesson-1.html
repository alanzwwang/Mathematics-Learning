🚀 The Number That Shouldn't Exist

📖 Lesson 1 of 9

👋 Start123456789🏋️ Practice🏆 Quiz

📌 Key idea of this section

Squaring a real number NEVER gives a negative.
So x² = −1 has no real answer...
until we invent one: i, with the single rule i² = −1.

The wall of squaring

Try to square your way to a negative number. Go ahead — try!

3² = 9           (−3)² = 9
(1/2)² = 1/4     0² = 0

Positive times positive is positive. Negative times negative is positive. Zero times zero is zero. There is simply no way — no real number can square to −1. For most of human history, that was the end of the story: x² = −1 was called impossible.

Invented numbers, a proud tradition

But here's a secret: mathematicians have invented their way out of "impossible" many times before.

  • ·
    "What is 3 − 5?" Once upon a time: impossible! Then NEGATIVE numbers were invented — and suddenly owing money made sense. People called them absurd.
  • ·
    "What is 1 ÷ 4?" Impossible, if only whole numbers exist! Then FRACTIONS were invented — and sharing one pizza among four friends became a number: 1/4.
  • ·
    "What is x, if x² = −1?" Impossible... until the 1500s, when Italian mathematicians, racing to solve harder equations, dared to write down a new number.

Each invention felt fake at first — and each became as normal as breakfast. The pattern: when an equation has no answer, don't give up. Invent the answer.

The one rule

The new number is called i (short for "imaginary unit" — a terrible name, given as an insult, that stuck). It obeys exactly ONE special rule:

i² = −1

Everything else about i is ordinary arithmetic. It adds, it multiplies, it follows all the usual rules. It just also happens to be the answer to the impossible equation.

A complex number is any number with both kinds of parts:

3 + 2i   ←  real part: 3,   imaginary part: 2

If the imaginary part is 0, you're looking at an ordinary real number wearing a disguise: 7 = 7 + 0i. So the real numbers live INSIDE the complex numbers — the same way the whole numbers live inside the fractions. Nothing you knew is gone; the number world just got bigger.

✏️ Your Turn!

  • (a)
    What is the imaginary part of 5 − 4i? (Careful — signs come along for the ride!)
  • (b)
    Is 7 a complex number?
👀 Show the answers

Answers: (a) −4 — the minus sign is part of the imaginary part. (b) Yes: 7 = 7 + 0i. Every real number is a complex number with imaginary part 0.
