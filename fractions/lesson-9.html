📌 Everything, one last time

Add/Subtract: match the bottoms, then combine the TOPS only
Multiply: straight across — top × top, bottom × bottom
Divide: keep, change, flip (that's multiplying by the reciprocal)
Simplify: divide top and bottom by their GCF (or cancel shared factors)
Solve: undo operations one legal move at a time — or clear fractions with the LCD
Inequalities: × or ÷ by a negative flips the sign

The recap list

· A fraction is division: a/b = a ÷ b. A rational number is anything writable as a/b with b ≠ 0.

· A minus sign has three homes; two minus signs cancel: (−3)/(−4) = 3/4.

· Zero on the bottom is forbidden — always.

· Equivalent fractions: multiply or divide top and bottom by the same nonzero number — even by x.

· Simplify with the GCF: 12/16 = 3/4. Or with factoring: (4x + 8)/4 = x + 2.

· Compare by cross-multiplying: 2/3 vs 3/5 → 10 > 9 → 2/3 > 3/5.

· Add/Subtract: LCD first, then the tops only — signs and variables included: x/2 + x/3 = 5x/6.

· Multiply: straight across, cancel early if you can: (−2/3)(−3/4) = 1/2.

· Exponents: xᵃ · xᵇ = xᵃ⁺ᵇ, and (2/3)² = 4/9.

· Divide: keep, change, flip — and you saw the proof: 2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 2/3 × 5/4 = 5/6.

· Equations: undo in reverse order, or clear fractions by multiplying every term by the LCD.

· Functions: f(x) = (3/4)x − 2 is a machine; f(8) = 4.

The magic sentence (honors remix)

Match the bottoms to add —
go straight across to multiply —
flip the second to divide —
and clear the fractions to solve.

Say it out loud three times. That's the whole lesson in four lines.

Capstone 1: Two methods, one answer

Solve: (2/3)x + 2 = x − 2. There's more than one legal road!

Method 1 — clear the fraction first:
Multiply both sides by 3:   2x + 6 = 3x − 6
Subtract 2x from both sides:   6 = x − 6
Add 6 to both sides:   x = 12
Method 2 — collect the x's first:
Subtract (2/3)x from both sides:   2 = x − (2/3)x − 2
Combine like terms (x − (2/3)x = (1/3)x):   2 = (1/3)x − 2
Add 2 to both sides:   4 = (1/3)x
Multiply both sides by 3:   x = 12
Check: (2/3)(12) + 2 = 8 + 2 = 10  and  12 − 2 = 10 ✅

Two different roads, same destination. In algebra that's normal — what matters is that every step is legal on both sides.

Capstone 2: Systems — two equations at once

A system of equations is two equations with two unknowns; a solution is a pair (x, y) that makes BOTH true at the same time.

Method: substitution (replace x with what it equals)
x + y = 5     and     x = (2/3)y
Substitute into the first equation:   (2/3)y + y = 5
Combine like terms ((2/3)y + (3/3)y):   (5/3)y = 5
Multiply both sides by 3/5:   y = 3
Then x = (2/3)(3) = 2
Check: 2 + 3 = 5 ✅  and  2 = (2/3)(3) ✅
Method: elimination (add the equations to make a variable vanish)
a + b = 7     and     a − b = 3
Add the left sides, add the right sides (equals + equals are equal):
(a + b) + (a − b) = 7 + 3
2a = 10   →   a = 5
Back-substitute: 5 + b = 7, so b = 2
Check: 5 − 2 = 3 ✅

Capstone 3: A peek at quadratics

Solve: x² = 25/16
(5/4)² = (5/4)(5/4) = 25/16 ✅
But also (−5/4)² = (−5/4)(−5/4) = 25/16 ✅
Two answers! We write x = ±5/4 (the symbol ± means "plus or minus").
Squaring erases the sign — so an x² equation usually has TWO solutions.
Geometry version: a square has area 9/4. Its side s satisfies s² = 9/4,
so s = ±3/2 — but a LENGTH can't be negative, so s = 3/2.
And one more peek: multiplying out (x + 2)(x + 3) by distributing:
(x + 2)(x + 3) = x² + 3x + 2x + 6 = x² + 5x + 6.
Undoing THAT — turning x² + 5x + 6 back into (x + 2)(x + 3) — is
called factoring a quadratic, and it's waiting for you in Algebra II.

Why this matters

Fractions aren't a topic you finish — they're the water algebra swims in. Slope is rise ÷ run: a fraction. Probability is favorable ÷ total: a fraction. Every rate, every ratio, every equation with a coefficient — fractions. And you now own the machinery: signs, LCDs, reciprocals, clearing, undoing. That's real algebra, and you're doing it.
