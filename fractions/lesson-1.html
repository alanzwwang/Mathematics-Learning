🚀 Fractions Are Division — Meet the Rational Numbers

📖 Lesson 1 of 9

👋 Start123456789🏋️ Practice✅ Answers

📌 Key idea of this section

A fraction is division wearing a bar:  a/b  means  a ÷ b.
Any number that can be written as a/b (with b ≠ 0) is a RATIONAL number.

The fraction bar is a division sign

What is 3 ÷ 4? Split 3 pizzas among 4 people: each person gets 3/4 of a pizza. The division problem 3 ÷ 4 and the fraction 3/4 are the same answer wearing two outfits:

3 ÷ 4 = 3/4        7 ÷ 2 = 7/2        1 ÷ 3 = 1/3

The top number has a name — the numerator — and the bottom has a name — the denominator. (Memory trick: the denominator is down under, and it names the denomination — the size of the pieces.) Once you see the bar as division, a lot of mystery evaporates.

Every integer is secretly a fraction. 5 = 5/1, because 5 ÷ 1 = 5. Same with negatives: −7 = −7/1. Any number that can be written as a/b — an integer on top, a nonzero integer on the bottom — is called a rational number. ("Rational" comes from "ratio"!) Integers, fractions, and negative fractions are all rational numbers.

The minus sign has three homes

Where does a negative sign live in a fraction? In any of three places — and all three mean the same number:

−(3/4)  =  (−3)/4  =  3/(−4)

Why? Because each one is a division with exactly one negative: (−3) ÷ 4 and 3 ÷ (−4) are both negative, since a negative divided by a positive (or a positive divided by a negative) is negative. One minus sign anywhere → the whole fraction is negative. Two minus signs → they cancel: (−3)/(−4) = 3/4, because negative ÷ negative = positive.

The one rule that can never break: b ≠ 0

The bottom of a fraction can be any number except zero. Why? The fraction 5/0 would have to mean 5 ÷ 0 — and 5 ÷ 0 would be a number q with q × 0 = 5. But anything times 0 is 0, never 5. No such q exists, so 5/0 is undefined — not a number at all. Flip it around and there's no problem: 0/5 = 0, because 0 = 0 × 5. Zero on top is fine; zero on the bottom is forbidden. This rule will follow you through all of algebra — whenever a variable shows up on the bottom, we'll whisper "as long as that bottom isn't zero."

Fractions on the number line — now with negatives

You may already know that 3/4 sits between 0 and 1: split the space from 0 to 1 into 4 equal hops and take 3. Negative fractions work the same way, mirrored to the left of 0. Where is −5/4? Since 5/4 = 1 + 1/4, the point −5/4 sits one quarter-hop to the LEFT of −1 — between −2 and −1, closer to −1.

There's always another fraction between two fractions

Here's a genuinely amazing fact about rational numbers: between any two of them, no matter how close, there is always another one. The trick: average them. The average of two numbers is their sum divided by 2 — and the average always sits exactly halfway between them.

Between 1/3 and 1/2:
Step 1: add them:        1/3 + 1/2 = 2/6 + 3/6 = 5/6
Step 2: divide by 2:     5/6 ÷ 2 = 5/12
Check: 1/3 = 4/12  <  5/12  <  6/12 = 1/2   ✅

No gaps, ever. Mathematicians call this property density — the rational numbers are dense.
