📌 Keep these next to you while you work

Add/Subtract: match the bottoms first — then the TOPS only
Multiply: straight across (top × top, bottom × bottom)
Divide: keep the first, change ÷ to ×, flip the second
Solve: undo operations — or multiply every term by the LCD
Inequalities: × or ÷ by a negative FLIPS the sign

Grab a pencil and paper. Start with the green ones — they use the exact patterns from the lessons. A few problems are open-ended: they have more than one right answer.

Hint for every problem: first ask yourself, "Which tool does this need — a sign rule, an LCD, a reciprocal, an undo, or a flip?"

🔴 CHALLENGE (Problems 86–100)

  • 86.
    Backwards problem. The answer is x = 6. INVENT an equation of the form
    (a/b)x + c = d whose solution is x = 6 — then show that it works.
  • 87.
    Two methods, one answer. Solve (5/6)x − 1 = 2/3 twice:
    (a) by clearing fractions first (multiply every term by 6),
    (b) by adding 1 first, then multiplying by the reciprocal.
    Which road felt shorter?
  • 88.
    System by substitution. Solve: x + y = 7 and x = (3/4)y.
  • 89.
    System by elimination. Solve: a + b = 9 and a − b = 1.
    Bonus: check whether the larger number is 5/4 of the smaller.
  • 90.
    Quadratic peek. Solve x² = 9/25. (Two answers! Why?)
  • 91.
    Square garden. A square garden has area 16/9 m². (a) How long is one
    side? (b) What is the perimeter?
  • 92.
    In-between hunt — with negatives. Find TWO different fractions between
    −1/2 and −1/3. Prove each one is really between them.
  • 93.
    Mystery fraction — the negative twist. I am a fraction equivalent to
    3/4. My top and bottom add up to −14. What am I?
  • 94.
    The painter. A painter paints 2/5 of a room per hour. (a) How many
    hours does the whole room take? (b) How much of the room is painted
    after 3/4 of an hour?
  • 95.
    The average trick. The average of two numbers is 3/4, and one of the
    numbers is 1/2. What is the other number? (Average = sum ÷ 2.)
  • 96.
    Function machine, twice! Let f(x) = (2/3)x. Compute f(f(9)) — that
    means plug f(9) back into f. (Work from the inside out.)
  • 97.
    Design it — two ways. (a) Invent a word problem whose answer comes
    from 2 ÷ 2/5. (b) Invent a word problem that turns into the equation
    (3/4)x = 12. Then solve both to make sure they work!
  • 98.
    Explain the mistake. A classmate says (x + 4)/2 = x + 2. Show they're
    wrong with a test value, then write the correct simplified form and
    explain, in one sentence, why the canceling they did is illegal.
  • 99.
    Egyptian fractions, upgraded. (a) Show 7/12 = 1/3 + 1/4. (b) Find two
    DIFFERENT unit fractions (top = 1) that add up to 3/8.
  • 100.
    The grand finale — build the whole machine. Invent an equation of
    the form (p/q)x + r = s (with p/q a fraction) whose solution is a
    whole number. Solve it step by step. Then write a word problem that
    your equation models. You are officially the teacher now!

🎉 You finished the whole honors lesson! If you can solve these 100 problems, you don't just "know fractions" — you can simplify them with variables inside, compare them with one cross-multiplication, solve equations and inequalities with them, run them through functions, and even crack a system or a simple quadratic. That's the engine room of algebra, and it's yours now. The next time you see a fraction inside an equation, smile: you have the reciprocal key, the LCD eraser, and the famous flip. Great work!
