🎯 Comparing Fractions — Cross-Multiplication and the Inequality Flip

📖 Lesson 3 of 9

👋 Start123456789🏋️ Practice✅ Answers

📌 Key idea of this section

To compare a/b and c/d (with positive bottoms), cross-multiply:
compare a·d against c·b. And in an inequality, multiplying or
dividing by a NEGATIVE number flips the sign.

The old way: common denominators

Which is bigger, 2/3 or 3/5? The classic move: rewrite both with a common bottom — the LCD (least common denominator), which is the least common multiple of the bottoms. LCM(3, 5) = 15:

2/3 = 10/15         3/5 = 9/15
10/15 > 9/15,  so  2/3 > 3/5   ✅

The one-step shortcut: cross-multiplication

Look at what the common-bottom move actually compares. Using bottom 15 = 3·5:

2/3 = (2·5)/(3·5) = (2·5)/15        3/5 = (3·3)/(3·5) = (3·3)/15

The bottoms match automatically — so the whole contest is between the two cross-products 2·5 = 10 and 3·3 = 9. That's the shortcut:

To compare a/b and c/d (bottoms positive):  compute a·d and c·b.
If a·d > c·b, then a/b > c/d.

One multiplication on each side, no LCD needed: 10 > 9, so 2/3 > 3/5. Done in one line.

Comparing with negatives

Move minus signs up into the tops first (Lesson 1!), then cross-multiply carefully:

Which is bigger, −2/3 or −3/5?  Write them as (−2)/3 and (−3)/5.
Cross-products:  (−2)·5 = −10   and   (−3)·3 = −9.
−10 < −9  (with negatives, the bigger-looking number is smaller!)
So −2/3 < −3/5.  Number-line check: −2/3 sits left of −3/5. ✅

Into algebra: solving inequalities

The symbols: < (less than), > (greater than), ≤ (less than or equal to), ≥ (greater than or equal to), ≠ (not equal to). An inequality with a variable asks: which numbers make this true?

x/3 < 2
Multiply both sides by 3 (positive, so the sign stays):   x < 6
Check x = 6: 6/3 = 2 — not less than 2, correctly excluded.
Check x = 3: 3/3 = 1 < 2 ✅

The famous flip

Here's the rule that trips up half the world. Start with a true fact and multiply both sides by −1:

2 < 4   is true.  Multiply both sides by −1:  −2 ... −4.
On the number line, −2 is to the RIGHT of −4, so  −2 > −4.

Multiplying by a negative reflects the number line — the order flips. So:

THE RULE: multiplying or dividing both sides of an inequality
by a negative number flips the sign (< becomes >, ≤ becomes ≥).
−3x ≥ 12
Divide both sides by −3 — negative! Flip:   x ≤ −4
Check x = −5: −3(−5) = 15 ≥ 12 ✅
Check x = 0: −3(0) = 0, and 0 ≥ 12 is false — correctly excluded. ✅

✏️ Your Turn!

  • (a)
    Which is bigger, −5/6 or −7/8? (Cross-multiply!)
  • (b)
    Solve: x/4 ≤ −2. (Multiplying by positive 4 — no flip.)
  • (c)
    Solve: −2x > 10. (Careful...)
👀 Show the answers

Answers: (a) Write as (−5)/6 and (−7)/8. Cross-products: (−5)·8 = −40 and (−7)·6 = −42. Since −40 > −42, we get −5/6 > −7/8. (b) Multiply both sides by 4: x ≤ −8. (c) Divide both sides by −2 and FLIP: x < −5. Check x = −6: −2(−6) = 12 > 10 ✅.
