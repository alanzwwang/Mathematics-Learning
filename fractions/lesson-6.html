🌟 Dividing — Keep, Change, Flip (and This Time We Prove It)

📖 Lesson 6 of 9

👋 Start123456789🏋️ Practice🏆 Quiz

📌 Key idea of this section

Dividing by a fraction = multiplying by its reciprocal.
KEEP the first, CHANGE ÷ to ×, FLIP the second.
2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 2/3 × 5/4 = 10/12 = 5/6

The reciprocal

The reciprocal of a fraction is the fraction flipped: the reciprocal of 4/5 is 5/4, and the reciprocal of −2/3 is −3/2 (the sign stays put!). The key fact:

A fraction times its reciprocal is always 1:   (4/5)(5/4) = 20/20 = 1.

The move — and why it works

You know the move: 2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 2/3 × 5/4 = 5/6. But WHY does flipping work? Here is a full proof, with every step explained. Call the answer A:

Let A = (a/b) ÷ (c/d).
Step 1: Division undoes multiplication, so A is THE number with
        A · (c/d) = a/b.          (that sentence is what "÷" means)
Step 2: Multiply both sides by d/c:
        A · (c/d) · (d/c) = (a/b) · (d/c)
Step 3: (c/d)(d/c) = 1, so the left side collapses:
        A · 1 = (a/b)(d/c)
Step 4: A · 1 = A, so:
        A = (a/b)(d/c)   ∎

Dividing by c/d gives exactly the same thing as multiplying by d/c — no exceptions, for any fractions with b, c, d ≠ 0. The little square ∎ means "proof complete." You just read a real mathematical proof!

Signs and variables come along

−3/4 ÷ 2/3 = −3/4 × 3/2 = −9/8        (negative × positive = negative)
(x/2) ÷ (x/3) = (x/2)(3/x) = 3x/(2x) = 3/2    (as long as x ≠ 0)

Complex fractions: fractions over fractions

A complex fraction is a fraction whose top or bottom is itself a fraction. The long bar is just division in disguise, so rewrite it as ÷ and keep-change-flip:

(2/3)/(4/5) = 2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 2/3 × 5/4 = 5/6
(3/4)/6     = 3/4 ÷ 6 = 3/4 × 1/6 = 3/24 = 1/8
(2/3)/(x/6) = 2/3 ÷ x/6 = (2/3)(6/x) = 12/(3x) = 4/x   (x ≠ 0)
