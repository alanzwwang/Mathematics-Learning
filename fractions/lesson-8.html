⚠️ Watch Out! Common Mistakes (Honors Edition)

📖 Lesson 8 of 9

👋 Start123456789🏋️ Practice🏆 Quiz

📌 Keep the big ideas in sight

Match the bottoms to add. Go straight across to multiply.
Flip the second to divide. Flip the INEQUALITY when you × or ÷ by a negative.

These six mistakes cost students points every single year — now in algebra flavors. Learn them now, and they can't catch you!

Mistake 1: Adding the bottoms

1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5?   ❌
The algebra twin:  x/2 + x/3 = x/5?   ❌

Catch it with a test value! Plug in x = 6: the left side is 3 + 2 = 5; the right side is 6/5 — nowhere close. Match the bottoms first: x/2 + x/3 = 3x/6 + 2x/6 = 5x/6. ✅

Mistake 2: Canceling TERMS instead of FACTORS

(x + 2)/2 = x + 1?   ❌

You can't cancel the bottom 2 with the 2 inside a sum — the bottom divides the WHOLE top. Test x = 4: (4 + 2)/2 = 3, but 4 + 1 = 5. Busted. The honest split:

(x + 2)/2 = x/2 + 2/2 = x/2 + 1   ✅

Canceling is fine only when the thing you cancel is a FACTOR of the entire top: (2x)/2 = x works, because the top is 2·x.

Mistake 3: Flipping the wrong fraction

2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 3/2 × 4/5?   ❌

Keep, change, flip — flip the SECOND fraction, the one you're dividing by. The first stays exactly as it is: 2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 2/3 × 5/4 = 5/6. ✅ (Flipping BOTH is wrong too — one flip only!)

Mistake 4: Forgetting the inequality flip

−2x < 6   →   x < −3?   ❌

Dividing both sides by −2 is dividing by a NEGATIVE — the sign must flip: x > −3. ✅ Check x = 0: −2(0) = 0 < 6 is true, and 0 > −3 is true — consistent. The wrong answer x < −3 would exclude 0!

Mistake 5: Losing the parentheses on a squared negative

(−1/2)² = −1/4?   ❌

The exponent applies to EVERYTHING inside the parentheses: (−1/2)² = (−1/2)(−1/2) = 1/4. ✅ What IS negative is −(1/2)² = −(1/4) = −1/4 — without parentheses, the square lands on the 1/2 only. Parentheses decide who gets squared.

Mistake 6: Distributing to only one term

(2/3)(x + 6) = (2/3)x + 6?   ❌

The multiplier must visit EVERY term: (2/3)(x + 6) = (2/3)x + (2/3)(6) = (2/3)x + 4. ✅ Test x = 3: (2/3)(3 + 6) = (2/3)(9) = 6, and the correct split gives 2 + 4 = 6 — but the wrong version gives 2 + 6 = 8.
