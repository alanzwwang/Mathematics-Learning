🚀 Right Triangles — Meet the Mighty Hypotenuse

📖 Lesson 1 of 9

👋 Start123456789🏋️ Practice✅ Answers

📌 Key idea of this section

A right triangle has one 90° corner.
The side across from the right angle — the hypotenuse — is always the longest side.

You already know right triangles: they're triangles with one perfect square corner — a 90° angle, like the corner of a book. Trigonometry lives inside right triangles, so let's name every part.

· The two sides that FORM the right angle are called the LEGS. They're like the floor and the wall meeting in the corner of a room.

· The side ACROSS from the right angle is called the HYPOTENUSE (say "hy-POT-en-oos"). It's always slanted, like a ramp — and it's always the LONGEST side of the triangle. Always. No exceptions, ever.

Why is the hypotenuse always longest? The biggest side of any triangle always sits across from the biggest angle — and in a right triangle, the 90° angle IS the biggest angle. (The other two must squeeze into the leftover 90°, so each one is smaller than 90°.)

One more useful fact: a right triangle can never have TWO right angles. Two 90° angles would already eat up all 180° — leaving nothing for the third corner!

✏️ Your Turn!

  • (a)
    A right triangle has sides 5, 12, and 13. Which is the hypotenuse? (b) A right triangle has sides 7, 24, and 25. Which sides are the legs?
👀 Show the answers

Answers: (a) 13 — the longest side, across from the right angle. (b) 7 and 24 — the two shorter sides that form the right angle. The 25 is the hypotenuse.
