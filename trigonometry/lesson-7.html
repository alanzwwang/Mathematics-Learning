📌 Key idea of this section

height in the air     = sin(angle) × slanted length
reach across ground   = cos(angle) × slanted length
height from shadow    = tan(angle) × ground distance

Time for the payoff — using trig on the actual world. Every measuring problem has the same cast of characters: something SLANTED (a kite string, a ladder, a ramp — the hypotenuse), an ANGLE it makes with the ground, a HEIGHT (opposite the angle), and a GROUND distance (adjacent to the angle).

The recipes come straight from SOH CAH TOA. If sin = opposite ÷ hypotenuse, then flipping it around (just like "if 12 ÷ 3 = 4, then 12 = 4 × 3") gives: opposite = sin × hypotenuse. Same trick for cos and tan. Three recipes:

The kite. A kite string is 10 m long and makes a 30° angle with the ground. How high is the kite? Height is OPPOSITE the angle, string is the hypotenuse:

height = sin 30° × 10 = 0.5 × 10 = 5 m

The ladder. A 10 m ladder leans against a wall at a 60° angle with the ground. How far is its foot from the wall? That distance is ADJACENT to the angle:

distance = cos 60° × 10 = 0.5 × 10 = 5 m

The flagpole. A flagpole casts an 8 m shadow, and the sun's angle is 45°. How tall is it? Height is opposite, shadow is adjacent — that's tangent's job:

height = tan 45° × 8 = 1 × 8 = 8 m

Notice the strategy every single time: PICK the angle, NAME the sides you know and want, then choose the ratio that mentions exactly those two sides. That's the whole game.

    A ramp's slanted surface is 20 m long and rises at a 30° angle. How high does it reach? (b) A tree casts a 10 m shadow when the sun's angle has tan ≈ 0.58. How tall is the tree?
