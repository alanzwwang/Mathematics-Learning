🌟 The Special Angles — 30°, 45°, 60°

📖 Lesson 6 of 9

👋 Start123456789🏋️ Practice✅ Answers

📌 Key idea of this section — your permanent cheat sheet

sin 30° = 0.5       cos 30° ≈ 0.87      tan 30° ≈ 0.58
sin 45° ≈ 0.71      cos 45° ≈ 0.71      tan 45° = 1
sin 60° ≈ 0.87      cos 60° = 0.5       tan 60° ≈ 1.73

Every angle has its own sin, cos, and tan — its fingerprint, remember. Calculators know them all. But three angles are so famous and so useful that their values are worth memorizing: 30°, 45°, and 60°. That's the table above.

First, a symbol: ≈ means "about equal to." The true values of cos 30° and friends are decimals that go on forever, but 0.87 is so close that your ruler will never know the difference. Three values in the table are EXACT — sin 30° = 0.5 and cos 60° = 0.5 and tan 45° = 1 — which is why problems adore them.

Now hunt for the patterns hiding in the table (test-makers love these):

· Read down the sin column: 0.5, 0.71, 0.87. As the angle GROWS, sine GROWS.

· Read down the cos column: 0.87, 0.71, 0.5. As the angle grows, cosine SHRINKS.

· Spot the twins: sin 30° = cos 60°, and sin 60° = cos 30°. Partners add to 90° — and sine of one equals cosine of the other, just like Lesson 4 promised!

· tan 45° = 1 exactly. Why? A right triangle with a 45° angle has two EQUAL legs (its angles are 45°-45°-90°) — and opposite ÷ adjacent with equal legs is 1. Always.

· tan 60° ≈ 1.73 — bigger than 1! Tangent is allowed to do that. Sine and cosine never can.
