⚡ The Pythagorean Theorem — Find the Missing Side

📖 Lesson 5 of 9

👋 Start123456789🏋️ Practice✅ Answers

📌 Key idea of this section

leg² + leg² = hypotenuse²
3² + 4² = 5²   —   that's 9 + 16 = 25 ✅

Problem: sometimes a problem hands you only TWO sides of a right triangle — but trig needs all three! Luckily, the ancient Greeks (and Babylonians before them) discovered a beautiful rule hiding in every right triangle. Square each leg, add the squares, and you get the square of the hypotenuse:

leg² + leg² = hypotenuse²

This is the PYTHAGOREAN THEOREM (say "puh-thag-uh-REE-un"). Check it on the 3-4-5: 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5². It works!

Missing hypotenuse? Add the leg squares, then find what number squared gives that total:

Legs 5 and 12:  25 + 144 = 169. What squared is 169? 13! (13² = 169)

Missing leg? Subtract instead:

Leg 8, hypotenuse 10:  100 − 64 = 36. What squared is 36? 6! (6² = 36)

The hall-of-fame triples (whole-number side sets that work perfectly):

3-4-5 · 6-8-10 · 5-12-13 · 9-12-15 · 8-15-17

Now the full two-step move that the practice problems love: find the missing side FIRST, then compute the trig ratios. Legs 5 and 12? Hypotenuse is 13 (from above) — and now sin, cos, and tan of either angle are yours for the taking.

✏️ Your Turn!

A right triangle has legs 8 and 15. (a) Find the hypotenuse. (b) Then give sin of the angle facing side 8.

Answers: (a) 64 + 225 = 289, and 17² = 289 — the hypotenuse is 17. (b) sin = opposite ÷ hypotenuse = 8/17.
