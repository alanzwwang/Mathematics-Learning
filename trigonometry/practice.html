Grab a pencil and paper — and sketch! A quick triangle with the angle circled turns most trig problems from mysterious to obvious. Start with the easy ones — they use the exact same patterns from the lessons. A few problems are open-ended: they have more than one right answer. Don't peek at the answer key until you've tried!

Hint for every problem: PICK the angle, NAME the sides (opposite? adjacent? hypotenuse?), then choose SOH, CAH, or TOA.

🟢 EASY (Problems 1–70)

Problems 1–8 — Meet the right triangle! (Lesson 1)

    In a right triangle, what do we call the longest side — the one across

  • 2.

  • 3.
    True or false: the hypotenuse is always the longest side of a right
    triangle.
  • 4.
    A right triangle has sides 6, 8, and 10. Which side is the hypotenuse?
  • 5.
    A right triangle has sides 5, 12, and 13. Which sides are the legs?
  • 6.

  • 7.
    True or false: every triangle has a hypotenuse.
  • 8.
    One leg of a right triangle is 3 and the other leg is 4. Without knowing
    the exact hypotenuse: is it bigger than 4 or smaller than 4?

  • 10.

  • 11.

  • 12.

  • 13.

  • 14.

  • 15.

  • 16.
    A new triangle has legs 5 and 12 and hypotenuse 13. Angle C faces the

Problems 27–34 — Whose ratio is it? (Lesson 4)

Setup for 27–34: a right triangle has legs 6 and 8 and hypotenuse 10.
Angle X faces side 6, and angle Y faces side 8.
  • 27.
    A student computed 6/10. Which ratio is that: sin X, cos X, or tan X?
  • 28.
    Another student computed 8/10. Which ratio is that?
  • 29.
    A third student computed 6/8. Which ratio is that?
  • 30.
    Simplify 6/10. Which famous triangle has the same angles as this 6-8-10
    triangle? What does that tell you about their ratios?
  • 31.
    Simplify 8/10.
  • 32.
    Which is bigger, sin X or cos X? (Compare your simplified fractions.)
  • 33.
    Is tan X bigger than 1 or smaller than 1? How do you know?
  • 34.
    Now think about angle Y. Is tan Y bigger than 1 or smaller than 1?

Problems 51–60 — Measure the world! (Lesson 7)

  • 51.
    A kite string is 10 m long and makes a 30° angle with the ground.
    Height = sin 30° × 10. How high is the kite? (sin 30° = 0.5)
  • 52.
    A 10 m ladder leans against a wall at a 60° angle with the ground.
    Distance from the wall = cos 60° × 10. Find it. (cos 60° = 0.5)
  • 53.
    A ramp's slanted surface is 20 m long and rises at a 30° angle.
    How high does it reach? (sin 30° = 0.5)
  • 54.
    A flagpole casts an 8 m shadow when the sun is at 45°.
    Height = tan 45° × 8. How tall is it? (tan 45° = 1)
  • 55.
    A tree casts a 10 m shadow when the sun's angle has tan ≈ 0.58.
    About how tall is the tree?
  • 56.
    A kite string is 20 m long and makes a 60° angle with the ground.
    About how high is the kite? (sin 60° ≈ 0.87)
  • 57.
    A slide is 10 m long along its slope and meets the ground at 30°.
    How tall is the high end? (sin 30° = 0.5)
  • 58.
    A roof rises 1 m for every 1 m across (that's tan 45° = 1).
    How much does it rise over 6 m across?
  • 59.
    A gentle ramp rises 0.58 m for every 1 m across (that's tan 30° ≈ 0.58).
    How much does it rise over 10 m across?
  • 60.
    A ladder leaning at 60° reaches 8.7 m up a wall. Since
    height on the wall = sin 60° × ladder length, the ladder's length is
    8.7 ÷ 0.87. How long is the ladder?

Problems 79–82 — Compare and connect! (Lessons 3–4)

  • 79.
    Triangle 1 has legs 3 and 4 with hypotenuse 5. Triangle 2 has legs 6
    and 8 with hypotenuse 10. In each triangle, compute sin of the angle
    that faces the smaller leg (simplify both!). What do you notice?
  • 80.
    Order these three from least to greatest: tan of the angle facing
    side 3 in a 3-4-5 triangle, tan 45°, and tan of the angle facing
    side 4 in a 3-4-5 triangle.
  • 81.
    In any right triangle, the two pointy angles add to 90°. If
    sin A = 5/13, what is cos B (the OTHER pointy angle)? Explain why
    in one sentence.
  • 82.
    sin 20° ≈ 0.34. Without any table or calculator, what is cos 70°?
    How do you know?

Problems 83–86 — Word problems with two steps! (Lesson 7)

  • 83.
    The kite. A kite string is 20 m long. (a) At a 30° angle, how high is
    the kite? (sin 30° = 0.5) (b) The wind lifts the string to a 60° angle
    with the same 20 m of string. About how high now? (sin 60° ≈ 0.87)
  • 84.
    The ladder. A 10 m ladder leans at a 60° angle against a wall.
    (a) How far is its foot from the wall? (cos 60° = 0.5)
    (b) About how high up the wall does it reach? (sin 60° ≈ 0.87)
  • 85.
    The ramp law. A ramp may rise at most 0.58 m per meter across
    (that's tan 30° ≈ 0.58). (a) The ramp covers 10 m across — what's the
    greatest rise allowed? (b) Is that ramp steeper or gentler than a
    45° ramp? How do you know?
  • 86.
    The two poles. Pole 1 casts a 6 m shadow at 45° (tan 45° = 1).
    Pole 2 casts a 10 m shadow at 30° (tan 30° ≈ 0.58). Find both heights.
    Which pole is taller?

Problems 87–88 — Explain yourself!

  • 87.
    Why can sin and cos never be bigger than 1, but tan can? Answer in
    one or two sentences. (Hint: think about what's on the bottom of each
    fraction.)
  • 88.
    In a 3-4-5 triangle, sin A = 3/5 for the angle A facing side 3. Without
    computing anything new, what is cos of the OTHER pointy angle?
    Explain why in one sentence.

Problems 89–90 — Spot the impossible!

  • 89.
    A student wrote: "In this triangle, sin A = 5/4." Explain why that's
    impossible — without even seeing the triangle.
  • 90.
    A triangle has legs 5 and 12 and hypotenuse 13. A student computed
    tan A = 5/13 for the angle A facing side 5. What did they do wrong,
    and what is the correct tan A?

🔴 CHALLENGE (Problems 91–100)

  • 91.
    The isosceles split. An isosceles triangle has two sides of 5 and a
    base of 6. Split it straight down the middle into two identical right
    triangles. (a) Each right triangle has one leg of 3 (half the base) and
    hypotenuse 5 — find the other leg (the triangle's height!). (b) Find
    sin, cos, and tan of a BASE angle (the angle where the base meets a
    side of 5 — it faces the height you just found).
  • 92.
    Steepest ramp — open-ended! A ramp rises 3 m over 4 m across, so its
    tan is 3/4. Invent a second ramp that is STEEPER and a third ramp that
    is GENTLER. Give the tan of each as a fraction, and order all three
    ramps by steepness.
  • 93.
    The twin kites. Kite 1 has a 10 m string at a 30° angle. Kite 2 has a
    20 m string at the same 30° angle. (a) Find both heights.
    (sin 30° = 0.5) (b) Kite 2's string is double, and its height is
    double — so why is its sin NOT double? Explain!
  • 94.
    The 45-45 triangle. A right triangle has two EQUAL legs. (a) What are
    its two pointy angles? (Hint: they share the leftover 90° equally.)
    (b) Explain why tan 45° must be EXACTLY 1. (c) Explain why
    sin 45° = cos 45°.
  • 95.
    Build-a-triangle — open-ended! Describe TWO different right triangles
    in which tan A = 3/4 (give all three sides of each triangle).
    Check each one.
  • 96.
    The shrinking shadow. The sun climbs from 30° in the morning to 60°
    at noon. (a) Does tan get bigger or smaller as the sun climbs? Use the
    cheat sheet. (b) Shadows work like this: shadow = height ÷ tan. If tan
    gets bigger, does a shadow get longer or shorter? (c) A 6 m pole at
    45° casts a shadow of 6 ÷ 1 = 6 m. Is its morning shadow (at 30°)
    longer or shorter than 6 m? What about its noon shadow (at 60°)?
  • 97.
    Which hill is steeper? Hill A rises 5 m over 12 m across. Hill B rises
    8 m over 15 m across. Compare their tans — you'll need a common
    denominator! Which hill is steeper?
  • 98.
    Partner angles. sin 25° ≈ 0.42 and cos 25° ≈ 0.91. (a) What is
    cos 65°? (b) What is sin 65°? (c) Explain the trick in one sentence.
    (Hint: 25° + 65° = 90°.)
  • 99.
    Design-a-problem — open-ended! Invent one word problem whose answer
    uses sin 30° = 0.5, and another whose answer uses tan 45° = 1.
    Solve both to make sure they work. (Use problems 51–58 as models.)
  • 100.
    The grand finale — the surveyor's day. A surveyor measures three
    things in one afternoon: (a) A 20 m zip-line cable makes a 30° angle
    with the ground. How high is the start above the end? (sin 30° = 0.5)
    (b) A 20 m crane arm points up at a 60° angle. How far OUT (across the
    ground) does it reach? (cos 60° = 0.5) (c) A building casts a 20 m
    shadow while the sun's angle has tan = 3/4. How tall is the building?
    (d) Which ratio — sin, cos, or tan — did each part use?
