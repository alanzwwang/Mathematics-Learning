🧩 Opposite and Adjacent — It's All About Where You Stand

📖 Lesson 2 of 9

👋 Start123456789🏋️ Practice🏆 Quiz

📌 Key idea of this section

Pick an angle. The side ACROSS from it is the OPPOSITE.
The side NEXT TO it (that isn't the hypotenuse) is the ADJACENT.
Switch angles — the labels switch too!

Here's a game. Take any right triangle and pick one of its two pointy corners (not the right angle — one of the other two). Stand at that corner and look around:

  • ·
    The side directly ACROSS from you — the one your corner doesn't touch at all — is the OPPOSITE side. Think: you're at home plate, and the opposite side is the outfield wall, way across the field.
  • ·
    The side right NEXT TO you — one of the two sides touching your corner — that ISN'T the hypotenuse is the ADJACENT side. ("Adjacent" means "next to," like adjacent seats in a movie theater.)
  • ·
    The hypotenuse is just the hypotenuse. It touches your corner too, but it never gets a new name. It's above this labeling game — it's the hypotenuse forever.

Now for the twist that makes this a real skill: switch to the OTHER pointy corner, and the opposite and adjacent sides SWAP. The side that was across from corner A is next to corner B!

Example: a right triangle has legs 3 and 4, and hypotenuse 5. Angle A faces the side of length 3. So for angle A: opposite = 3, adjacent = 4. But angle B faces the side of length 4 — for angle B: opposite = 4, adjacent = 3. Same triangle, different angle, different labels!

✏️ Your Turn!

A right triangle has legs 6 and 8, and hypotenuse 10. Angle C faces the side of length 8. (a) For angle C, name the opposite and adjacent sides. (b) Now switch to angle D, which faces the side of length 6. Name the opposite and adjacent sides.

👀 Show the answers

Answers: (a) Opposite = 8 (across from C), adjacent = 6 (next to C, not the hypotenuse). (b) Opposite = 6, adjacent = 8 — they swapped! The hypotenuse stayed 10, as always.
