Intermediate

  • 71.
    (a) 5 (9 + 16 = 25). (b) sin A = 3/5, cos A = 4/5, tan A = 3/4 ✅
  • 72.
    (a) 13 (25 + 144 = 169). (b) sin B = 12/13, tan B = 12/5 ✅
  • 73.
    (a) 8 (100 − 36 = 64). (b) sin = 6/10 = 3/5, cos = 8/10 = 4/5 ✅
  • 74.
    (a) 8 (289 − 225 = 64). (b) tan = 15/8 ✅
  • 75.
    10 — the opposite doubled (3 → 6), so the hypotenuse doubles (5 → 10)
  • 76.
    12 — the opposite tripled (3 → 9), so the adjacent triples (4 → 12)
  • 77.
    24 — the hypotenuse doubled (13 → 26), so the adjacent doubles (12 → 24)
  • 78.
    16 — the hypotenuse doubled (17 → 34), so the opposite doubles (8 → 16)
  • 79.
    3/5 and 6/10 = 3/5 — identical! Same angle, same ratio, even though
    triangle 2 is double the size. That's the Lesson 3 big idea.
  • 80.
    3/4 < 1 < 4/3 — the small-leg angle, then 45°, then the big-leg angle.
  • 81.
    cos B = 5/13. The side opposite A is adjacent to B, so A's sine and
    B's cosine are the same fraction.
  • 82.
    cos 70° ≈ 0.34 — 20° and 70° are partners (they add to 90°), and
    sin of one partner equals cos of the other.
  • 83.
    (a) 0.5 × 20 = 10 m. (b) 0.87 × 20 = 17.4 m — steeper angle,
    bigger sine, higher kite!
  • 84.
    (a) 0.5 × 10 = 5 m from the wall. (b) 0.87 × 10 = 8.7 m up the wall.
  • 85.
    (a) 0.58 × 10 = 5.8 m. (b) Gentler — its tan (0.58) is less than
    tan 45° (1), and a smaller tan means a gentler slope.
  • 86.
    Pole 1: 1 × 6 = 6 m. Pole 2: 0.58 × 10 = 5.8 m. Pole 1 is taller —
    but barely! The longer shadow made pole 2 LOOK taller.
  • 87.
    Sin and cos put the hypotenuse — always the LONGEST side — on the
    bottom, so the top can never win. Tan divides leg by leg, and either
    leg can be longer, so tan can top 1.
  • 88.
    cos of the other angle = 3/5. The side that's OPPOSITE angle A is
    ADJACENT to the other pointy angle — same fraction, new job.
  • 89.
    sin = opposite ÷ hypotenuse, and the hypotenuse is always the longest
    side — so sine can never reach 1, let alone 5/4. A sine bigger than 1
    would need the opposite side to be longer than the longest side.
    Impossible!
  • 90.
    They used the hypotenuse — but tangent never touches the hypotenuse!
    TOA: tan = opposite ÷ adjacent = 5/12.

Challenge

  • 91.
    (a) 3² + h² = 5², so h² = 25 − 9 = 16, and h = 4.
    (b) The base angle faces the height: opposite = 4, adjacent = 3,
    hypotenuse = 5. So sin = 4/5, cos = 3/5, tan = 4/3. It's the 3-4-5
    triangle hiding inside an isosceles triangle! ✅
  • 92.
    Sample: steeper ramp = rise 4 over 3 across (tan = 4/3); gentler ramp =
    rise 3 over 5 across (tan = 3/5). Order: 3/5 < 3/4 < 4/3. Any ramp
    whose tan beats 3/4 is steeper; any whose tan loses to 3/4 is gentler.
  • 93.
    (a) Kite 1: 0.5 × 10 = 5 m. Kite 2: 0.5 × 20 = 10 m.
    (b) sin 30° = 0.5 in BOTH kites. The ratio depends only on the angle,
    not the size — doubling every side doubles the height but leaves every
    fraction unchanged. Same angle, same ratio! ✅
  • 94.
    (a) 45° each — they split the leftover 90° equally (and equal legs
    mean equal angles across from them).
    (b) tan = opposite ÷ adjacent, and with equal legs that's the same
    number over itself — always exactly 1.
    (c) sin and cos both have the hypotenuse on the bottom; with equal
    legs, their tops are equal too — so sin 45° = cos 45°.
  • 95.
    Any 3-4-5 family triangle works: 3-4-5 (tan of the angle facing
    side 3 is 3/4 ✅) and 6-8-10 (tan = 6/8 = 3/4 ✅) — or 9-12-15
    (9/12 = 3/4 ✅). Did you find others?
  • 96.
    (a) Bigger — tan climbs from ≈0.58 (at 30°) to ≈1.73 (at 60°).
    (b) Shorter — you're dividing by a bigger number, so the shadow
    shrinks. (c) At 30°, tan < 1, so height ÷ tan is LONGER than 6 m;
    at 60°, tan > 1, so it's SHORTER than 6 m. High sun, short shadows!
  • 97.
    Hill A: tan = 5/12 = 25/60. Hill B: tan = 8/15 = 32/60.
    32/60 beats 25/60 — Hill B is steeper. Same trick as comparing any
    fractions: common denominators don't lie. ✅
  • 98.
    (a) cos 65° ≈ 0.42. (b) sin 65° ≈ 0.91. (c) 25° and 65° are partners
    (they add to 90°), so sin of one equals cos of the other — you can
    swap sin and cos whenever the two angles add to 90°.
  • 99.
    Sample sin problem: "A zip-line is 12 m long and makes a 30° angle
    with the ground. How high is the start?" → 0.5 × 12 = 6 m.
    Sample tan problem: "A flagpole casts a 7 m shadow at 45°. How tall
    is it?" → 1 × 7 = 7 m. Yours may be completely different — if one
    multiplies a slant by 0.5 and the other multiplies a shadow by 1,
    you're right!
  • 100.
    (a) height = sin 30° × 20 = 0.5 × 20 = 10 m.
    (b) reach = cos 60° × 20 = 0.5 × 20 = 10 m.
    (c) height = tan × shadow = 3/4 × 20 = 15 m.
    (d) Sine for the zip-line (opposite from slant), cosine for the crane
    (adjacent from slant), tangent for the building (opposite from
    ground distance). Three measurements, three ratios — a full trig day! ✅

If you can solve these 100 problems, you truly understand trigonometry — how to name the sides of a right triangle from any angle, why the same angle always owns the same ratios, how sine, cosine, and tangent turn those ratios into measurements, and how a shadow plus an angle can measure anything from a kite to a building. The next time you see a kite, a ladder, or a long afternoon shadow, do a little trig in your head. Great work!
