Same thing to both sides. Opposite move. Reverse order. Respect the signs. Flip on negative multiply/divide. Check!

Grab a pencil and paper. Start with the green ones — they use the exact patterns from the lessons. The yellows mix the patterns together. The reds ask you to reason, design, and explain. Don't peek at the answer key until you've tried!

Hint for every problem: first ask yourself, "What was done to x — and what is the opposite move?"

Problems 89–94: Backwards, design, and explain — the honors gym.

  • 89.
    Backwards problem: the machine f(x) = 5x − 4 just produced the output 26. What was the input? Explain each step.
  • 90.
    The answer is x = 7. BUILD a two-step equation whose solution is 7, and explain how you built it. (Hint: dress 7 up in two layers, then write the dressed-up version as the left side.)
  • 91.
    The answer is x = −4. Build an equation that has x on BOTH sides whose solution is −4. Verify that your equation works.
  • 92.
    Design an equation that has NO solution, and explain in one sentence why no x can ever solve it.
  • 93.
    Design an equation that has INFINITELY many solutions, and explain in one sentence why every x works.
  • 94.
    Two methods, one answer: solve 6(x − 2) = 24 by (A) dividing first and by (B) distributing first. Then explain why both methods are legal.

Problems 95–98: Quadratic peeking (Lessons 7 and 11).

  • 97.

  • 98.
    A square garden has an area of 64 square meters. Write an equation for the side length s and solve it. Your equation has two solutions — which one is the real answer, and why do we reject the other?
  • 99.

  • 100.
    The grand finale. A rectangle's perimeter is 36 cm. Its length is 4 cm more than its width. (a) Write an equation using w for the width. (b) Solve it. (c) State both the width and the length, and check that the perimeter really comes out to 36.
