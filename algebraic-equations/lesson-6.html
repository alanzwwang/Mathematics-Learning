🌟 x on Both Sides — and Two Sneaky Special Cases

📖 Lesson 6 of 12

👋 Start123456789101112🏋️ Practice✅ Answers

📌 KEY IDEA

Collect all the x's on one side first (Golden Rule moves only!), then unwrap as usual. And if the x's ever vanish completely, what remains tells you the truth.

The Main Move

Look at this:

7x − 4 = 3x + 12

There are x's at both ends of the seesaw. Before unwrapping, we gather them together. The move: subtract the smaller x-group from both sides.

Worked Example 1: 7x − 4 = 3x + 12

7x − 4 − 3x = 3x + 12 − 3x   subtract 3x from both sides (Golden Rule)
4x − 4 = 12               7x − 3x = 4x; 3x − 3x cancels
4x = 16                   add 4 to both sides
x = 4                     divide both sides by 4

Check: left: 7 × 4 − 4 = 24. right: 3 × 4 + 12 = 24. ✅

Worked Example 2: 5(x − 2) = 2x + 8

5x − 10 = 2x + 8          distribute the 5
3x − 10 = 8               subtract 2x from both sides
3x = 18                   add 10 to both sides
x = 6                     divide both sides by 3

Check: left: 5 × (6 − 2) = 20. right: 2 × 6 + 8 = 20. ✅

Worked Example 3: (1/2)x + 3 = (3/4)x − 1 (clear the fractions!)

Honors trick: when fractions annoy you, multiply EVERY term on BOTH sides by a number that wipes the denominators out. Here, 4 does the job:

4 × (1/2)x + 4 × 3 = 4 × (3/4)x − 4 × 1   multiply every term by 4
2x + 12 = 3x − 4          4 × 1/2 = 2; 4 × 3/4 = 3 — fractions gone!
12 = x − 4                subtract 2x from both sides
16 = x                    add 4 to both sides

It is perfectly fine for x to end up on the right — the equal sign doesn't care. So x = 16.

Check: left: (1/2)(16) + 3 = 11. right: (3/4)(16) − 1 = 12 − 1 = 11. ✅

Special Case 1: Infinitely Many Solutions

Watch what happens here:

2(x + 3) = 2x + 6
2x + 6 = 2x + 6           distribute the 2
6 = 6                     subtract 2x from both sides — the x's VANISH!

Is 6 = 6 true? Always! It doesn't matter what x is — both sides are really the same expression wearing two costumes. An equation that is true for EVERY value of x is called an identity, and it has infinitely many solutions. Try x = 100: left: 2 × 103 = 206; right: 200 + 6 = 206. ✅

Special Case 2: No Solution

2(x + 3) = 2x + 5
2x + 6 = 2x + 5           distribute the 2
6 = 5                     subtract 2x from both sides — the x's vanish again!

But 6 = 5 is false — and no value of x can fix it, because x is gone! This equation has no solution. The two sides are parallel expressions that can never be equal.

How to Tell Them Apart

When your x's cancel out completely, look at the leftover number sentence:

Leftover is TRUE  (like 6 = 6)   →  infinitely many solutions (identity)
Leftover is FALSE (like 6 = 5)   →  no solution

This is not a weird exception — it is the math telling you the truth about the problem you started with.
