Problem Set

60 problems

Overview123456789101112ProblemsAssessment

The problems are arranged in three groups. The Core problems check fluency with the definitions and computations of each lesson. The Advanced problems ask for short proofs and multi-step computations. The Challenge problems are of the kind found on graduate qualifying examinations and require combining ideas from several lessons.

Core (Problems 1–20)

  • 1.
    Show that T(x, y) = (x + 2y, 3x − y) is linear on ℝ² and write down its matrix.
  • 2.
    Verify directly that f(x) = 5x − 2 satisfies f(λx + (1 − λ)y) = λf(x) + (1 − λ)f(y) for all real x, y, λ.
  • 3.
    Find the affine function f : ℝ → ℝ with f(1) = 4 and f(3) = 10.
  • 4.
    A linear map T : ℝ² → ℝ² sends (1, 0) to (2, 1) and (0, 1) to (−1, 3). Find its matrix and T(3, −2).
  • 5.
    Decide whether (1, 0, 1), (0, 1, 1), (1, 1, 0) is a basis of ℝ³.
  • 6.
    Find the dimension of U = {x ∈ ℝ⁴ : x₁ + x₂ + x₃ + x₄ = 0} and give a basis.
  • 7.
    Find the kernel, image, rank and nullity of the matrix with rows (1, −1) and (2, −2).
  • 8.
    A linear map ℝ⁶ → ℝ⁴ has a 3-dimensional kernel. What is its rank? Is it surjective?
  • 9.
    Write the matrix of differentiation D on P₃ with respect to (1, x, x², x³) and find its rank.
  • 10.
    A has rows (2, 1) and (1, 2), and B = P⁻¹AP for some invertible P. Find tr B, det B and the eigenvalues of B.
  • 11.
    Find the dual basis of b₁ = (2, 1), b₂ = (1, 1) in ℝ².
  • 12.
    Find the barycentric coordinates of (1, 2) with respect to A = (0, 0), B = (3, 0), C = (0, 3). Where does the point lie?
  • 13.
    Compute the Jacobian matrix and its determinant for f(x, y) = (x² − y², 2xy) at (1, 1).
  • 14.
    Use the linearisation of √x at 100 to estimate √101.
  • 15.
    Find the least-squares line for (1, 1), (2, 2), (3, 2) and its residuals.
  • 16.
    Find the matrix of the orthogonal projection of ℝ² onto the line spanned by (1, 2).
  • 17.
    Maximise 4x + 3y subject to 2x + y ≤ 10, x + y ≤ 7, x, y ≥ 0.
  • 18.
    Write the dual of: maximise 2x₁ + 3x₂ subject to x₁ + x₂ ≤ 4, x₁ + 3x₂ ≤ 6, x ≥ 0. Solve both.
  • 19.
    Find the Lipschitz constant of f(x) = 3|x| − 2x on ℝ.
  • 20.
    Show that ReLU(x) − ReLU(−x) = x and ReLU(x) + ReLU(−x) = |x|.

Advanced (Problems 21–45)

  • 21.
    Let T : V → V be linear with dim V = n and T² = 0. Prove that rank T ≤ n/2.
  • 22.
    Prove that rank(AB) ≤ min(rank A, rank B).
  • 23.
    Prove Sylvester's inequality: for A of size m × n and B of size n × p, rank A + rank B − n ≤ rank(AB).
  • 24.
    Let T(p)(x) = p(x + 1) on P₂. Find the matrix of T with respect to (1, x, x²) and the matrix of T⁻¹.
  • 25.
    Let A have rows (5, −6) and (3, −4). Diagonalise A and compute A¹⁰.
  • 26.
    Let φ be a linear functional on n × n matrices with φ(AB) = φ(BA) for all A, B. Prove that φ = c·tr for some scalar c.
  • 27.
    Prove that dim U⁰ = dim V − dim U for a subspace U of a finite-dimensional space V.
  • 28.
    Let t₀, …, tₙ be distinct reals and evᵢ(p) = p(tᵢ). Prove that ev₀, …, evₙ is a basis of Pₙ* and identify the dual basis in Pₙ.
  • 29.
    Find w and a > 0 such that ∫₋₁¹ p(t) dt = w(p(−a) + p(a)) for every p ∈ P₃.
  • 30.
    Let T : V → W have matrix M with respect to bases B, C. Prove that T* : W* → V* has matrix Mᵀ with respect to the dual bases.
  • 31.
    Prove that an affine map preserves ratios along lines: if z = x + t(y − x) then f(z) = f(x) + t(f(y) − f(x)).
  • 32.
    Find the affine map sending (0, 0), (1, 0), (0, 1) to (1, 1), (3, 2), (0, 4), and the factor by which it multiplies areas.
  • 33.
    Show that x ↦ Ax + b corresponds to the (n + 1) × (n + 1) block matrix with rows (A, b) and (0, 1), and that composition corresponds to matrix multiplication.
  • 34.
    Let f(x, y) = xy/(x² + y²) for (x, y) ≠ (0, 0) and f(0, 0) = 0. Show that both partial derivatives exist at the origin but f is not differentiable there.
  • 35.
    Let f(x, y) = (x + y, xy) and g(u, v) = u² − 2v. Compute D(g∘f)(1, 2) with the chain rule and check it directly.
  • 36.
    Perform two steps of Newton's method for x² + y² = 4, x = y, starting at (1, 1).
  • 37.
    Prove that ker(AᵀA) = ker A and hence rank(AᵀA) = rank A for every real matrix A.
  • 38.
    Fit y = c₀ + c₁x + c₂x² by least squares to (−1, 2), (0, 0), (1, 1), (2, 4).
  • 39.
    Let A have full column rank and P = A(AᵀA)⁻¹Aᵀ. Prove that Pᵀ = P, P² = P, and tr P = rank A.
  • 40.
    Prove that in least-squares regression with an intercept, the residuals sum to zero and the fitted line passes through (x̄, ȳ).
  • 41.
    In the model yᵢ = βxᵢ + εᵢ with Gauss–Markov errors, compare the variances of β̃ = ȳ/x̄ and the OLS estimator β̂ = Σxᵢyᵢ/Σxᵢ². Evaluate the ratio for x = (1, 2, 3).
  • 42.
    Maximise 5x + 4y subject to 6x + 4y ≤ 24, x + 2y ≤ 6, −x + y ≤ 1, y ≤ 2, x, y ≥ 0. Find an optimal dual solution.
  • 43.
    Use Farkas' lemma to show that x₁ + x₂ + x₃ = 2, 2x₁ + 3x₂ + x₃ = 1 has no solution with x ≥ 0, and give a certificate.
  • 44.
    Find the Lipschitz constant of x ↦ Ax + b on ℝ² when A has rows (1, 2) and (2, 1).
  • 45.
    Write the tent function max(0, 1 − |x|) as a combination of ReLUs.

Challenge (Problems 46–60)

  • 46.
    Prove that ℝ is infinite-dimensional as a vector space over ℚ.
  • 47.
    Let f : ℝ → ℝ be additive but not of the form f(x) = cx. Prove that the graph of f is dense in ℝ².
  • 48.
    Prove that no n × n matrices satisfy AB − BA = I, and exhibit linear operators D, M on the space of all polynomials with DM − MD = I.
  • 49.
    Let V have an infinite basis (eᵢ)i∈I, with coordinate functionals φᵢ. Prove that the φᵢ are linearly independent but do not span V*.
  • 50.
    Let f : ℝⁿ → ℝⁿ satisfy ‖f(x) − f(y)‖ = ‖x − y‖ (Euclidean norm). Prove that f is affine.
  • 51.
    For the Newton iteration xₖ₊₁ = (xₖ + 2/xₖ)/2, prove the exact identity xₖ₊₁ − √2 = (xₖ − √2)²/(2xₖ) and deduce quadratic convergence for x₀ > 0.
  • 52.
    Two unbiased measurements y₁, y₂ of μ are uncorrelated with variances 1 and 4. Find the BLUE of μ and its variance, and compare with the plain average.
  • 53.
    Let H be the hat matrix of a regression with p parameters. Prove that each leverage hᵢᵢ satisfies 0 ≤ hᵢᵢ ≤ 1 and that Σhᵢᵢ = p.
  • 54.
    Derive the complementary slackness conditions from the proof of weak duality.
  • 55.
    Find optimal mixed strategies and the value of the zero-sum game whose payoff matrix to the row player has rows (3, −1) and (−2, 1).
  • 56.
    Find the line y = a + bx that minimises the maximum absolute error at (0, 0), (1, 1), (2, 0), formulate the problem as an LP, and prove optimality.
  • 57.
    Prove that every continuous piecewise-linear function on ℝ is a difference of two convex piecewise-linear functions, and do this explicitly for the hat function of Lesson 12.
  • 58.
    Compute the operator norm of the shear with rows (1, 1) and (0, 1).
  • 59.
    On the space P of all real polynomials, show that p ↦ xp is injective but not surjective and that p ↦ p′ is surjective but not injective. Why does this not contradict rank–nullity?
  • 60.
    Prove that f : ℝⁿ → ℝ is affine if and only if it is both convex and concave.
