Problem Set

60 problems

Overview123456789101112ProblemsAssessment

The problems are grouped by difficulty. Core problems check the definitions and the main formulas of each lesson; Advanced problems require combining several results or giving a short proof; Challenge problems are proofs of the kind found on graduate qualifying examinations. Coordinates refer to ℝ² or ℂ unless stated otherwise, and in Ceva and Menelaus problems all ratios are signed.

Core (Problems 1–20)

  • 1.
    Classify the isometry T(x, y) = (−y + 3, x + 1) and find its fixed point.
  • 2.
    Classify T(x, y) = (x + 2, −y + 4); if it is a glide reflection, give its axis and glide vector.
  • 3.
    Identify the composite σ₂σ₁, where σ₁ is the reflection in y = 0 and σ₂ the reflection in y = 3.
  • 4.
    Identify the composite of the half-turn about (1, 2) followed by the half-turn about (4, −1).
  • 5.
    Find the order of the symmetry group of a regular octagon and the number of reflections it contains.
  • 6.
    Can a lattice in ℝ² be invariant under the rotation through 45°? Justify with a trace argument.
  • 7.
    For the triangle with sides 5, 12, 13, compute R, r and OI².
  • 8.
    For the same triangle compute OH² from Corollary 5.2 and explain the answer geometrically.
  • 9.
    Find the radius of the nine-point circle of the triangle with sides 13, 14, 15.
  • 10.
    In triangle ABC, BD/DC = 3/2 and CE/EA = 4/5. Find AF/FB for which AD, BE, CF are concurrent.
  • 11.
    A transversal meets BC at D with BD/DC = −2 and AB at F with AF/FB = 3/4. Find CE/EA where it meets CA.
  • 12.
    Find the power of (7, 1) with respect to the circle with centre (2, 1) and radius 3, and the tangent length.
  • 13.
    Find the radical axis of x² + y² = 16 and (x − 10)² + y² = 36 and explain the result.
  • 14.
    Invert the point (1, 2) in the circle x² + y² = 25.
  • 15.
    Compute the cross-ratio (A, B; C, D) for the points 1, 2, 4, 8 of the real line.
  • 16.
    Find the line through the points [1 : 2 : 1] and [3 : 0 : 1] of ℝP² and its affine equation.
  • 17.
    Verify Euler's formula for the pentagonal prism.
  • 18.
    Compute the angular defect at a vertex of the regular dodecahedron and check Descartes's theorem.
  • 19.
    Decide whether the regular 51-gon and the regular 25-gon are constructible.
  • 20.
    Prove that ∛5 is not constructible.

Advanced (Problems 21–45)

  • 21.
    Let a, b, c be the lines containing the sides of a triangle. Prove that σc σb σa is a glide reflection, not a reflection.
  • 22.
    Find the axis and glide vector of the opposite isometry T(z) = i z̄ + 1 + 3i of ℂ.
  • 23.
    In ℂ, let ρ₀ be the rotation through 60° about 0 and ρ₁ the rotation through 120° about 1. Identify ρ₁ρ₀ and find its centre.
  • 24.
    Prove that the n reflections of Dₙ form a single conjugacy class if n is odd and two classes if n is even.
  • 25.
    Prove that no wallpaper group contains both a rotation of order 3 and a rotation of order 4.
  • 26.
    For B = (0, 0), C = (8, 0), A = (2, 6) compute O, G, H and verify OG : GH = 1 : 2 and OH² = 9R² − (a² + b² + c²).
  • 27.
    For the triangle of Problem 26 find the centre and radius of the nine-point circle.
  • 28.
    Compute OI for the triangle with sides 6, 8, 10.
  • 29.
    Compute the Routh area ratio when BD/DC = CE/EA = AF/FB = 4.
  • 30.
    Compute the Routh area ratio for x = 1, y = 2, z = 5.
  • 31.
    In the 13–14–15 triangle (a = 14, b = 15, c = 13), find the three cevian ratios of the Gergonne point and verify Ceva.
  • 32.
    Find the image of the line x + y = 2 under inversion in the unit circle.
  • 33.
    Find the image of the circle (x − 3)² + y² = 4 under inversion in the unit circle.
  • 34.
    A cyclic quadrilateral ABCD has AB = 2, BC = 3, CD = 4, DA = 5. Find AC · BD.
  • 35.
    Find the radical centre of x² + y² = 1, (x − 4)² + y² = 9 and x² + (y − 6)² = 4, and its common power.
  • 36.
    Show that when (A, B; C, D) = −1, the six values of the cross-ratio under permutations reduce to three, and list them.
  • 37.
    Find the harmonic conjugate of C = 3 with respect to A = 1 and B = 7.
  • 38.
    Count the points, lines and incident point–line pairs of the projective plane PG(2, 3).
  • 39.
    The cuboctahedron has 8 triangular and 6 square faces, with 4 faces at each vertex. Find V and E and verify Euler's formula.
  • 40.
    Prove that a convex polyhedron all of whose faces are triangles satisfies E = 3V − 6 and F = 2V − 4.
  • 41.
    A torus is subdivided into a 4 × 4 grid of quadrilaterals. Compute V − E + F.
  • 42.
    Derive the dihedral angle of the regular octahedron from sin(δ/2) = cos(π/q)/sin(π/p).
  • 43.
    Compute the order of the rotation group of the icosahedron in three ways using the orbit–stabiliser theorem.
  • 44.
    Is the box 1 × 2 × 4 scissors congruent to the cube of edge 2? Justify.
  • 45.
    Show that y = 2cos(2π/9) satisfies y³ − 3y + 1 = 0 and deduce that the regular 9-gon is not constructible.

Challenge (Problems 46–60)

  • 46.
    Prove that an isometry of ℝ² commuting with every translation is itself a translation.
  • 47.
    Prove that the centre of the group E(2) is trivial.
  • 48.
    Prove that a group of isometries containing non-trivial rotations about two distinct points contains a non-trivial translation.
  • 49.
    Prove a² + b² + c² ≤ 9R² for every triangle and deduce sin²A + sin²B + sin²C ≤ 9/4.
  • 50.
    Use Euler's relation to compute OI for the 3–4–5 triangle, and prove that R = 2r only for equilateral triangles.
  • 51.
    Prove that the reflection of the orthocentre H in the midpoint of BC is the point of the circumcircle diametrically opposite A.
  • 52.
    Prove that the composite of the inversions in two concentric circles of radii k₁ and then k₂ is the dilation by (k₂/k₁)².
  • 53.
    Find a point L such that inversion centred at L maps the circles x² + y² = 1 and (x − 5)² + y² = 4 to concentric circles.
  • 54.
    Prove that a projective transformation of ℝP² fixing four points in general position is the identity.
  • 55.
    Prove |PGL(3, q)| = q³(q³ − 1)(q² − 1) and that this equals the number of ordered quadrangles in PG(2, q). Evaluate for q = 2 and q = 3.
  • 56.
    The 24-cell has 24 octahedral cells, with 3 cells around each edge. Derive E and F and verify V − E + F − C = 0 given V = 24.
  • 57.
    A regular tetrahedron of edge 2 is cut into four regular tetrahedra of edge 1 and one regular octahedron of edge 1. Check that volume and Dehn invariant are additive for this dissection.
  • 58.
    Prove that cos 72° = (√5 − 1)/4 and conclude that the regular pentagon is constructible.
  • 59.
    Prove that the acute angles of the 3–4–5 right triangle are irrational multiples of π.
  • 60.
    Determine how many regular n-gons with 3 ≤ n ≤ 100 are constructible, and show that the 255-gon is constructible.
