Problem Set

60 problems

Overview12345678910111213ProblemsAssessment

The problems are grouped by difficulty. The Core problems check definitions and standard computations from each lesson. The Advanced problems require combining several ideas or writing short proofs. The Challenge problems are proofs of the kind found on graduate qualifying examinations; several of them complete arguments that the lessons only sketched. Unless stated otherwise, k is an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0 and curves are considered in ℙ²(k).

Core (Problems 1–20)

  • 1.
    Determine V(x² + y² − 1, y − 1) ⊂ 𝔸²(ℝ).
  • 2.
    Describe the algebraic set V(x² − y²) ⊂ 𝔸².
  • 3.
    Determine V(xy, x − 1) ⊂ 𝔸².
  • 4.
    Compute √⟨x³, y²⟩ in k[x, y].
  • 5.
    Is ⟨x² + 1⟩ a maximal ideal of ℝ[x]? Of ℂ[x]? Describe V(x² + 1) in each case.
  • 6.
    Find the irreducible components of V(x² − xy) ⊂ 𝔸².
  • 7.
    Find the rational point on x² + y² = 1 with parameter t = 3 in the parametrization through (−1, 0).
  • 8.
    Write down the primitive Pythagorean triples given by (m, n) = (4, 1), (5, 2) and (7, 4).
  • 9.
    Find the singular points of y² = x⁴ − x² and describe the real curve near each.
  • 10.
    Find the tangent line to the curve x³ + y³ = 9 at the point (1, 2).
  • 11.
    Homogenize y = x³ − 2x + 5 and find its points at infinity.
  • 12.
    Find the points at infinity of the hyperbola x² − 4y² = 1 and relate them to its asymptotes.
  • 13.
    A projective quartic and a projective cubic have no common component. How many intersection points do they have, counted with multiplicity?
  • 14.
    Compute I₀(y − x³, y).
  • 15.
    Decide whether y² = x³ − x and y² = x³ − 3x + 2 are smooth.
  • 16.
    Compute the j-invariants of y² = x³ + x and y² = x³ + 5.
  • 17.
    On y² = x³ + 1, compute (2, 3) + (0, 1).
  • 18.
    What is the genus of a smooth plane quintic?
  • 19.
    What is the genus of the curve y² = x⁵ − x?
  • 20.
    Identify the coordinate ring of the parabola V(y − x²) and the image of the function y under your isomorphism.

Advanced (Problems 21–45)

  • 21.
    Show that ℤ² is Zariski dense in 𝔸²(ℂ).
  • 22.
    Let k be infinite. Show that any two nonempty Zariski open subsets of 𝔸ⁿ intersect.
  • 23.
    Determine V(x − y², x² − y²) and decide whether the ideal J = ⟨x − y², x² − y²⟩ is radical.
  • 24.
    Find an explicit Nullstellensatz certificate showing that x² + y² − 1, x − 2 and y have no common zero.
  • 25.
    Find the smallest N with (x + y)ᴺ ∈ ⟨x², y²⟩ in ℚ[x, y].
  • 26.
    Show that φ(t) = (t² − 1, t(t² − 1)) maps 𝔸¹ onto the nodal cubic C: y² = x²(x + 1), is injective except that φ(1) = φ(−1), and is not an isomorphism.
  • 27.
    Show that the coordinate ring of the cuspidal cubic y² = x³ is not a unique factorization domain.
  • 28.
    Parametrize the conic x² + xy + y² = 1 by lines through (1, 0).
  • 29.
    Parametrize the rational points of x² + y² = 2 by lines through (1, 1), and find the point with t = 2.
  • 30.
    Find all intersection points, with multiplicities, of the circles x² + y² = 4 and (x − 1)² + (y − 1)² = 2 in ℙ²(ℂ).
  • 31.
    Compute I₀(y² − x³, y − x²) and compare it with the product of multiplicities.
  • 32.
    The projective closures of y² = x³ and y = x³ meet with total multiplicity 9. Find all intersection points and their multiplicities.
  • 33.
    Show that the projective closure of the twisted cubic { (t, t², t³) } ⊂ 𝔸³ meets the plane at infinity in exactly one point.
  • 34.
    Show that O = [0 : 1 : 0] is a flex of every Weierstrass cubic Y²Z = X³ + aXZ² + bZ³.
  • 35.
    On y² = x³ + 17, compute 2Q for Q = (−1, 4).
  • 36.
    Find all points of order 2 on y² = x³ − x and show that together with O they form a group isomorphic to ℤ/2 × ℤ/2.
  • 37.
    Count the points of y² = x³ + x + 1 over 𝔽₇ and determine the group structure.
  • 38.
    An irreducible plane quartic has exactly one singular point, an ordinary node. What is its geometric genus?
  • 39.
    Show that the Fermat quartic X⁴ + Y⁴ = Z⁴ is smooth and find its genus.
  • 40.
    Find the genus of the smooth projective curve y² = x⁷ − x.
  • 41.
    Show that no smooth plane curve has genus 2.
  • 42.
    Find the points at infinity of the projective closure of xy = 1 and the tangent lines there.
  • 43.
    Show that y² = x³ − x + 1 is smooth over ℚ, compute its j-invariant, and find the primes p ≥ 5 at which its reduction is singular.
  • 44.
    Find the tangent line to y² = x³ + 17 at P = (−2, 3) and its third intersection with the curve.
  • 45.
    On y² = x³ + 17 with P = (−2, 3), compute 3P.

Challenge (Problems 46–60)

  • 46.
    Show that U = 𝔸² ∖ {(0, 0)} is not isomorphic to an affine algebraic set. (A regular function on an open set is one that is locally a quotient of polynomials with nonvanishing denominator.)
  • 47.
    Prove that every algebraic set, with its Zariski topology, is quasi-compact: every open cover has a finite subcover.
  • 48.
    Prove that an irreducible conic is smooth.
  • 49.
    Prove that a plane cubic with two singular points is reducible.
  • 50.
    Prove that an irreducible plane curve of degree d has at most (d − 1)(d − 2)/2 singular points.
  • 51.
    Show that the smooth conics y = x² and x² + y² = y meet only at the origin, with intersection multiplicity 4.
  • 52.
    Prove Bézout's theorem when one of the curves is a line.
  • 53.
    Find all points of order 3 on y² = x³ + b (b ≠ 0) over ℂ, and the rational ones for b = 1.
  • 54.
    Show that the kernel of multiplication by 2 on a complex elliptic curve has exactly 4 elements.
  • 55.
    Count the points of y² = x³ + 3 over 𝔽₇, compare with Hasse's bound, and determine the group.
  • 56.
    Using the Legendre form, show that λ = −1, 2 and 1/2 all give j = 1728, and find the λ with j = 0.
  • 57.
    Compute the genus of the curve y³ = x⁴ + 1 in two ways: by Riemann–Hurwitz for the map (x, y) ↦ x, and by the degree–genus formula.
  • 58.
    Let C = V(f) ⊂ 𝔸² and P = (a, b) ∈ C. Show that k-algebra homomorphisms k[x, y]/⟨f⟩ → k[ε]/⟨ε²⟩ lying over P correspond to vectors (u, v) with fₓ(P)u + fy(P)v = 0, and deduce that this space has dimension 1 at smooth points and 2 at singular points.
  • 59.
    Describe the fibers of Spec ℤ[i] → Spec ℤ over ⟨2⟩, ⟨3⟩ and ⟨5⟩.
  • 60.
    Show that Spec ℤ[x] contains a chain of three distinct prime ideals, so that its Krull dimension is at least 2.
