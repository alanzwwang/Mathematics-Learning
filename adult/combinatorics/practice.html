The problems below are grouped by difficulty. The Core problems check the basic techniques of each lesson; the Advanced problems require combining methods or writing a complete proof; the Challenge problems are of the kind found in graduate qualifying examinations.

Core (Problems 1–20)

  • 1.
    How many solutions does x₁ + x₂ + x₃ + x₄ = 20 have in nonnegative integers?
  • 2.
    How many solutions does x₁ + x₂ + x₃ + x₄ = 20 have with every xᵢ ≥ 2?
  • 3.
    How many compositions of 12 into exactly 5 positive parts are there?
  • 4.
    How many surjections are there from [7] onto [3]?
  • 5.
    Compute D₇, and compare D₇/7! with 1/e.
  • 6.
    How many integers in [1, 1000] are divisible by none of 2, 3 and 5?
  • 7.
    Solve aₙ = 5aₙ₋₁ − 6aₙ₋₂ with a₀ = 1, a₁ = 4.
  • 8.
    Find the coefficient of x⁵ in (1 − 2x)⁻³.
  • 9.
    Compute S(6, 3) both from the recurrence and from the explicit formula.
  • 10.
    Compute B₅ using the recurrence Bₙ₊₁ = ∑ₖ C(n, k)Bₖ.
  • 11.
    Find p(10), and the number of partitions of 10 into distinct parts.
  • 12.
    How many triangulations does a convex octagon have?
  • 13.
    How many binary necklaces of length 6 are there up to rotation?
  • 14.
    In how many ways can the faces of a cube be coloured with 3 colours, up to rotation?
  • 15.
    Prove that among any n + 1 integers chosen from [2n], two are coprime, and two are such that one divides the other.
  • 16.
    How many spanning trees does K₅ have?
  • 17.
    For which n does Kₙ have an Euler circuit?
  • 18.
    Evaluate ∑ₖ C(8, k)² by a combinatorial argument.
  • 19.
    Candidate A receives 7 votes and B receives 4. What is the probability that A is strictly ahead throughout a random count?
  • 20.
    In how many ways can 10 distinct books be given to 4 people so that each person gets at least one?

Advanced (Problems 21–45)

  • 21.
    Solve aₙ = 4aₙ₋₁ − 4aₙ₋₂ with a₀ = 1, a₁ = 6, and verify a₃.
  • 22.
    Solve aₙ = 2aₙ₋₁ + 3ⁿ with a₀ = 1 using generating functions.
  • 23.
    Using generating functions, count the ways to make 50 cents from coins of 1, 5, 10 and 25 cents.
  • 24.
    Prove that for n ≥ 1, Dₙ is the integer nearest to n!/e.
  • 25.
    Count the involutions of [7].
  • 26.
    Count the partitions of [6] with no singleton block, and give the exponential generating function.
  • 27.
    Prove xⁿ = ∑ₖ S(n, k)(x)ₖ, and check it for n = 4 and x = 3.
  • 28.
    An ordered set partition is a set partition with a linear order on its blocks. Count the ordered set partitions of [4], and find their EGF.
  • 29.
    Use Euler's pentagonal recurrence to compute p(12) from p(0), …, p(11).
  • 30.
    Find the number of partitions of 12 into at most 3 parts.
  • 31.
    A partition is self-conjugate if λ = λ′. Prove that self-conjugate partitions of n are equinumerous with partitions of n into distinct odd parts, and count those of 12.
  • 32.
    How many Dyck paths of semilength 5 touch the x-axis only at their endpoints?
  • 33.
    How many east–north lattice paths from (0, 0) to (8, 5) never rise above the line y = x?
  • 34.
    Count the 3-colour necklaces of length 7 up to rotation, and up to rotation and reflection.
  • 35.
    Find a formula for the number of k-colourings of the vertices of a square up to the dihedral group of order 8, and evaluate it at k = 3.
  • 36.
    Using the cycle index of Example 10.4, count the graphs on 4 vertices with exactly 2 edges, up to isomorphism.
  • 37.
    In how many ways can the vertices of a cube be coloured with 2 colours, up to rotation?
  • 38.
    What is the minimal length N such that every sequence of N distinct reals has a monotone subsequence of length 6? Give an extremal example of length N − 1.
  • 39.
    Prove R(3, 4) ≤ 9.
  • 40.
    Prove R(3, 3, 3) ≤ 17.
  • 41.
    Prove that the edge set of a k-regular bipartite graph is a disjoint union of k perfect matchings.
  • 42.
    How many perfect matchings does K₁₀ have?
  • 43.
    Prove that every graph with m edges has a bipartite subgraph with at least m/2 edges, and show that the constant 1/2 cannot be replaced by any c > 1/2 for all graphs.
  • 44.
    Use Erdős's theorem to show that R(10, 10) > 100.
  • 45.
    Show that some tournament on 5 vertices has at least 8 Hamiltonian paths.

Challenge (Problems 46–60)

  • 46.
    Find the coefficients of x¹² and x¹³ in ∏ₖ≥₁ (1 − xᵏ).
  • 47.
    A rooted forest on [n] is a set of rooted trees whose vertex sets partition [n]. Prove that there are (n + 1)ⁿ⁻¹ rooted forests on [n], and evaluate for n = 4.
  • 48.
    Count the connected labelled graphs on 4 vertices using the exponential formula.
  • 49.
    Evaluate ∑ₖ C(10, k)C(8, 6 − k) and prove the general identity with generating functions.
  • 50.
    Find the number of domino tilings of a 3 × 8 rectangle, deriving the recurrence.
  • 51.
    Evaluate the Hardy–Ramanujan estimate for p(100) and its relative error.
  • 52.
    Prove that R(4, 4) ≤ 18, and state what is needed to show R(4, 4) = 18.
  • 53.
    Prove that every k-uniform hypergraph with fewer than 2ᵏ⁻¹ edges is 2-colourable, and compare the bound with the true values m(2) = 3 and m(3) = 7.
  • 54.
    How many necklaces of 6 beads with exactly 2 black and 4 white beads are there up to rotation? Up to rotation and reflection?
  • 55.
    Use the hook length formula to count the standard Young tableaux of shape (4, 3, 1).
  • 56.
    Count the permutations of [5] in which i is never immediately followed by i + 1.
  • 57.
    Let X be the number of fixed points of a uniformly random permutation of [n], n ≥ 2. Find E[X] and Var(X).
  • 58.
    Show that the number of compositions of n into odd parts is Fₙ, and evaluate for n = 10.
  • 59.
    Count binary strings of length 12 with no two consecutive 1s, and find the exponential growth rate of the sequence.
  • 60.
    Compare C₁₀ with the asymptotic estimate 4ⁿ/(n3/2√π).
