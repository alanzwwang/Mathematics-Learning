Problem Set

60 problems

Overview123456789101112ProblemsAssessment

The sixty problems below are arranged in three groups. The Core problems check the definitions, computations and algorithms of the lessons; the Advanced problems require short proofs or multi-step calculations; the Challenge problems are of the kind found in graduate texts and qualifying examinations.

Core (Problems 1–20)

  • 1.
    Using only the recursive definitions of Lesson 1, compute 2 · 3, recording each step.
  • 2.
    Prove from the definitions that a + 1 = S(a) and 1 + a = S(a) for every a ∈ ℕ.
  • 3.
    Which of the pairs (0, 5), (5, 0), (10, 15), (8, 3) represent the same integer as (3, 8)?
  • 4.
    Compute [(2, 7)] · [(4, 1)] in ℤ = (ℕ × ℕ)/∼ and give its normal form.
  • 5.
    In the construction of ℚ, verify that (1, 2) ∼ (3, 6), and compute (1, 2) + (1, 3) and (1, 2) · (1, 3) as pairs.
  • 6.
    Find the quotient and remainder (with 0 ≤ r < b) for a = −47, b = 6 and for a = −100, b = 7.
  • 7.
    Find gcd(252, 198) by Euclid's algorithm and express it as 252x + 198y.
  • 8.
    Factor 360 into primes and find its number of positive divisors τ(360) and their sum σ(360).
  • 9.
    Find the last digit of 7²²².
  • 10.
    Find the inverse of 7 modulo 26.
  • 11.
    Solve the congruence 5x ≡ 3 (mod 12).
  • 12.
    Write 2026 in bases 2, 8 and 16.
  • 13.
    Convert 101101₂, 2201₃ and 7EA₁₆ to decimal.
  • 14.
    Write the repeating decimal 0.363636… as a fraction in lowest terms.
  • 15.
    Which of 1/40, 1/12, 7/80 and 3/14 have terminating decimal expansions?
  • 16.
    Prove that √3 is irrational using the fundamental theorem of arithmetic.
  • 17.
    State the machine epsilon and the unit roundoff of binary64, as powers of 2 and in decimal.
  • 18.
    Decode the binary32 word 0 10000001 10100000000000000000000.
  • 19.
    Explain precisely why 0.1 + 0.2 == 0.3 evaluates to false in binary64 arithmetic.
  • 20.
    Compute 12 × 34 by one level of Karatsuba's method with B = 10.

Advanced (Problems 21–45)

  • 21.
    Prove that if a, b ∈ ℕ and ab = 1, then a = b = 1.
  • 22.
    Prove the principle of strong induction from the well-ordering principle.
  • 23.
    Prove that the order on ℤ defined by [(a, b)] ≤ [(c, d)] ⟺ a + d ≤ b + c is well defined.
  • 24.
    In an ordered field, prove that for a, b > 0 we have a < b if and only if a² < b².
  • 25.
    Prove that no finite field can be ordered.
  • 26.
    In ℚ(t) with the ordering of Lesson 4, show that 1/t is positive and smaller than every positive rational number.
  • 27.
    Divide 11 + 3i by 1 + 2i in ℤ[i], giving q and r with N(r) < N(1 + 2i).
  • 28.
    Find gcd(x⁶ − 1, x⁴ − 1) in ℚ[x] by the Euclidean algorithm.
  • 29.
    How many trailing zeros does 1000! have?
  • 30.
    Compute v₃(50!) by Legendre's formula and confirm it with the digit-sum formula.
  • 31.
    Show that 2 and 1 + √−5 are irreducible in ℤ[√−5] and are not associates.
  • 32.
    Find all x with x ≡ 1 (mod 4), x ≡ 2 (mod 5), x ≡ 3 (mod 7).
  • 33.
    Compute 2²⁰²⁶ mod 101.
  • 34.
    Find the last three digits of 3⁴⁰².
  • 35.
    List the units and the zero divisors of ℤ/12ℤ, and check that their numbers are φ(12) and 12 − 1 − φ(12).
  • 36.
    Find the decimal periods of 1/17, 1/37 and 1/41.
  • 37.
    Prove the divisibility test for 11: n is divisible by 11 if and only if its alternating digit sum is.
  • 38.
    Write 25 and 100 in balanced ternary.
  • 39.
    Write 6 and −3 in base −2.
  • 40.
    Prove that the sum A + B of two Dedekind cuts is a cut.
  • 41.
    Let aₙ be the decimal truncation of √2 to n places. Show that (aₙ) is a Cauchy sequence of rationals.
  • 42.
    Find the largest finite binary32 number exactly and to five significant figures.
  • 43.
    What is the ulp (spacing of consecutive binary64 numbers) at 2026.0, and how many binary64 numbers lie in [1024, 2048)?
  • 44.
    Bound the relative error of the floating-point evaluation fl(x · fl(y + z)) for floating-point x, y, z with y + z ≠ 0.
  • 45.
    Compute the relative condition numbers of f(x) = √x, f(x) = x − 1 and f(x) = sin x, and say where each is ill-conditioned.

Challenge (Problems 46–60)

  • 46.
    Deduce the recursion theorem with a parameter from Theorem 1.3.
  • 47.
    Prove that the ideal (2, x) of ℤ[x] is not principal.
  • 48.
    Prove that there are infinitely many primes congruent to 3 modulo 4.
  • 49.
    Prove that gcd(2ᵐ − 1, 2ⁿ − 1) = 2gcd(m, n) − 1, and compute gcd(4095, 262143).
  • 50.
    Show that Euclid's algorithm on the pair (F(n+2), F(n+1)) takes exactly n division steps, so Lamé's bound is sharp.
  • 51.
    Prove that ℝ has exactly one ordering making it an ordered field, and deduce that the only field automorphism of ℝ is the identity.
  • 52.
    Show that the number of idempotents (x² = x) in ℤ/nℤ is 2ω(n), where ω(n) is the number of distinct primes dividing n. List the idempotents of ℤ/360ℤ by their count and of ℤ/10ℤ explicitly.
  • 53.
    Prove that every Cauchy sequence of rationals is bounded and that the product of two Cauchy sequences is Cauchy.
  • 54.
    Show that the implication x ≠ y ⇒ x ⊖ y ≠ 0 fails in binary64 if subnormal results are flushed to zero, using x = 1.5 × 2⁻¹⁰²² and y = 2⁻¹⁰²².
  • 55.
    The population variance of the data 10⁹ + 4, 10⁹ + 7, 10⁹ + 13, 10⁹ + 16 is to be computed. Compare the one-pass formula (1/n)Σxᵢ² − x̄² with the two-pass formula (1/n)Σ(xᵢ − x̄)² in binary64.
  • 56.
    Let a ≥ b ≥ 0 be floating-point numbers and s = a ⊕ b. Using the Sterbenz lemma, prove that s ⊖ a is computed exactly.
  • 57.
    Solve the Karatsuba recurrence T(1) = 1, T(n) = 3T(n/2) + cn exactly for n = 2ᵏ.
  • 58.
    Show that Toom-k multiplication has exponent logk(2k − 1), compute it for k = 2, 3, 4, and show that it tends to 1.
  • 59.
    Newton's iteration for 1/d is started with error |e₀| = 1/2. How many iterations are needed to guarantee 10⁶ correct bits?
  • 60.
    (Midy's theorem.) Let p be a prime, p ≠ 2, 5, such that the decimal period of 1/p is even, say 2k. Prove that 10ᵏ ≡ −1 (mod p) and that the two halves of the repeating block of 1/p add to 10ᵏ − 1. Check it for 1/7 and 1/17.
