Problem Set

60 problems

Overview123456789101112ProblemsAssessment

The sixty problems below are graded in three tiers. The Core problems check fluency with the formulas and computations of the course. The Advanced problems require short proofs or multi-step modelling. The Challenge problems are longer and connect several lessons; some are standard results from the literature that are worth proving once in full. Numerical answers should be given to the stated precision; logarithms are natural unless indicated otherwise.

Core (Problems 1–20)

  • 1.
    Solve y′ = 0.3y with y(0) = 40 and evaluate y(5) to two decimal places.
  • 2.
    Find the doubling time of a quantity obeying y′ = 0.035y, to two decimal places.
  • 3.
    A quantity decays exponentially from 800 to 200 in 12 hours. Find its half-life and its decay constant λ.
  • 4.
    Find the effective annual rate of a nominal 8% compounded quarterly.
  • 5.
    Find the force of interest δ equivalent to an effective annual rate of 6%, to six decimal places.
  • 6.
    Find the present value of 10,000 due in 8 years at force of interest δ = 0.045.
  • 7.
    Iodine-131 has a half-life of 8.02 days. What fraction of a sample remains after 30 days?
  • 8.
    Find the mean lifetime of a nuclide with half-life 10 minutes.
  • 9.
    Prove that 1 + x ≤ eˣ for every real x, with equality only at x = 0, using the mean value theorem.
  • 10.
    Order by asymptotic growth: 1.1ⁿ, n¹⁰, 2√n, n³, nln n.
  • 11.
    Solve y′ = −2y + 8 with y(0) = 1 and identify the equilibrium and its stability.
  • 12.
    An object at 100 °C is placed in a room at 25 °C and is at 70 °C after 5 minutes. Find its temperature after 15 minutes under Newton's law of cooling.
  • 13.
    For P′ = 0.3P(1 − P/500), P(0) = 50, compute P(10) to one decimal place.
  • 14.
    Find the equilibria of y′ = y² − 4 and classify their stability.
  • 15.
    Compute etA for A = [[0, 1], [0, 0]] and solve x′ = Ax with x(0) = (a, b).
  • 16.
    Classify the equilibrium at the origin of x′ = Ax for A = [[−3, 1], [1, −3]], and give its eigenvalues.
  • 17.
    Solve xₙ₊₁ = 1.04xₙ + 100 with x₀ = 0, and compute x₁₀ to two decimal places.
  • 18.
    Solve xₙ₊₂ = xₙ₊₁ + 2xₙ with x₀ = 0, x₁ = 3, and state the growth rate per step.
  • 19.
    For the offspring distribution p₀ = 0.3, p₁ = 0.2, p₂ = 0.5, find the mean m and the extinction probability q.
  • 20.
    An SIR epidemic has R₀ = 3 and recovery rate γ = 0.25 per day. Find the herd-immunity threshold, the early growth rate and the early doubling time.

Advanced (Problems 21–45)

  • 21.
    Use Grönwall's inequality to give a second proof that the solution of y′ = ky, y(0) = y₀ is unique.
  • 22.
    Prove that bₙ = (1 + 1/n)n+1 is strictly decreasing, and deduce (1 + 1/n)ⁿ < e < (1 + 1/n)n+1 for every n ≥ 1.
  • 23.
    For which effective rate i is the rule of 72 exact, that is, 100 i · ln 2/ln(1 + i) = 72? Give i to three significant figures.
  • 24.
    A sample's ¹⁴C activity is 45% of that of modern carbon. Compute its age using T½ = 5730 years and using the Libby half-life 5568 years, to the nearest year.
  • 25.
    In the chain A → B → C with λA = 0.1 and λB = 0.3 per hour, find the time t* at which NB is maximal and the exact value of NB(t*)/N₀.
  • 26.
    For an exponential lifetime with mean τ, compute P(T > 2τ) and P(T½ < T ≤ 2T½).
  • 27.
    A nuclide decays by two independent channels whose partial half-lives are 10 hours and 40 hours. Prove that its lifetime is exponential and find the overall half-life.
  • 28.
    Deduce from Theorem 4.1 that (ln n)/nε → 0 for every ε > 0.
  • 29.
    Find all positive integers n with n³ > 2ⁿ.
  • 30.
    Use Stirling's formula to show C(2n, n) ~ 4ⁿ/√(πn), and compare with the exact value for n = 10.
  • 31.
    Find the radius of convergence of ∑ Fₙ zⁿ, where Fₙ are the Fibonacci numbers.
  • 32.
    Let u ≥ 0 be continuous with u(t) ≤ 2 + ∫₀ᵗ u(s)/(1 + s) ds for t ≥ 0. Find the bound given by Grönwall's inequality.
  • 33.
    Solve y′ = −y + e−t, y(0) = 0, and explain why the solution is not a combination of pure exponentials.
  • 34.
    Show that every logistic solution with 0 < P₀ < K is symmetric about its inflection point: P(t* + s) + P(t* − s) = K.
  • 35.
    For P′ = 0.8P(1 − P/5000) − 600, find the equilibria and their stability, and the maximum sustainable yield.
  • 36.
    For constant-effort harvesting with r = 0.8 and K = 5000, compute the sustained yield at E = 0.3 and find the effort that maximizes yield.
  • 37.
    Verify that P(t) = K exp(ln(P₀/K) e−rt) solves the Gompertz equation and that its inflection value is K/e.
  • 38.
    Compute etA for A = [[0, −2], [2, 0]] and describe the trajectories, including their period.
  • 39.
    Compute etA for A = [[1, 1], [0, 2]].
  • 40.
    Show that the flow of x′ = Ax preserves area in the plane if and only if tr A = 0.
  • 41.
    For A = [[1, 0], [0, 0]] and B = [[0, 1], [0, 0]], compute eA eB, eB eA and eA+B, and confirm that all three differ.
  • 42.
    For the Leslie matrix L = [[0.5, 1.5], [0.6, 0]], find the dominant eigenvalue to four decimal places and the net reproductive number R₀.
  • 43.
    Prove that Fₙ is the nearest integer to φⁿ/√5 for all n ≥ 0.
  • 44.
    Find the extinction probability of a Galton–Watson process with Poisson(1.5) offspring, to three decimal places.
  • 45.
    For an SIR epidemic with R₀ = 1.8 starting from S(0) ≈ N, compute the final size and the peak prevalence.

Challenge (Problems 46–60)

  • 46.
    (Herschel–Maxwell.) Let f : ℝ → (0, ∞) be continuous and even, with f(x) f(y) = f(0) f(√(x² + y²)) for all x, y. Prove that f(x) = f(0) ecx² for some constant c, and that c < 0 if f is integrable.
  • 47.
    Solve y′ = y² with y(0) = y₀ > 0 and show that the solution blows up in finite time. More generally, show that y′ = f(y) with f positive and continuous on [y₀, ∞) reaches infinity in finite time if and only if ∫y₀∞ dy/f(y) < ∞, and explain why linear growth never blows up.
  • 48.
    For A = [[−1, M], [0, −1]] with M > 0, compute etA and the maximum over t ≥ 0 of its (1, 2) entry. Evaluate for M = 100.
  • 49.
    Prove the variance formula Var Zₙ = σ² mn−1(mⁿ − 1)/(m − 1) for a Galton–Watson process with m ≠ 1.
  • 50.
    For the critical geometric offspring distribution pk = 2−(k+1), show that the n-th iterate of f(s) = 1/(2 − s) is fₙ(s) = (n − (n − 1)s)/(n + 1 − ns), and deduce P(Zₙ > 0) = 1/(n + 1). Compare with Kolmogorov's estimate.
  • 51.
    For the SIR model with general initial values S₀, I₀ and R₀S₀/N > 1, prove that the peak prevalence is Imax = S₀ + I₀ − (N/R₀)(1 + ln(R₀S₀/N)).
  • 52.
    Derive the Lambert W form z = 1 + W(−R₀e−R₀)/R₀ of the final size, and explain which branch gives the nontrivial root.
  • 53.
    Prove that the Euler–Lotka equation has exactly one positive root ρ, that every other eigenvalue λ of the Leslie matrix satisfies |λ| ≤ ρ, and that ρ > 1 if and only if R₀ > 1.
  • 54.
    For the Leslie matrix with f = (0, 0, 6), s₁ = 1/2, s₂ = 1/3, show that L³ = I. What does this say about convergence to a stable age distribution, and which hypothesis of Theorem 8.1 fails?
  • 55.
    Count the binary words of length n with no two consecutive 1s, and those with no three consecutive 1s, and compute the topological entropy of each constraint.
  • 56.
    Let S and T be finite symmetric generating sets of a group G. Prove that there is C ≥ 1 with βS(n) ≤ βT(Cn) for all n, and deduce that having exponential growth does not depend on the generating set.
  • 57.
    For A = [[1, 1], [0, 1]], compute Aⁿ and verify Gelfand's formula lim ‖Aⁿ‖1/n = ρ(A) directly, even though ‖Aⁿ‖ is unbounded.
  • 58.
    A recursive algorithm satisfies T(n) = T(n − 1) + T(n − 3) + 1. Find the exponential base of its running time to four decimal places.
  • 59.
    Prove that (I + A/n)ⁿ → eA for every square matrix A, with error at most ‖A‖² e‖A‖/(2n).
  • 60.
    Show that Ln[1/2, 1] grows faster than every power of ln n and slower than nε for every ε > 0.
