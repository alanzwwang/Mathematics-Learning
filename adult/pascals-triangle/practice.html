Problem Set

60 problems

Overview1234567891011121314ProblemsAssessment

The problems are grouped by difficulty. Core problems test the definitions and the main formulas of each lesson; Advanced problems require combining two or more results or writing a short proof; Challenge problems are of the kind found in graduate qualifying examinations and research-level texts. Unless stated otherwise, n and k denote nonnegative integers, p denotes a prime, and C(α, k) is defined as in Lesson 1.

Core (Problems 1–20)

  • 1.
    Compute C(−4, 3) and C(3/2, 3).
  • 2.
    Evaluate ∑ₖ k C(12, k).
  • 3.
    Evaluate ∑k=06 (−1)ᵏ C(15, k) in closed form.
  • 4.
    Evaluate ∑m=520 C(m, 5).
  • 5.
    Evaluate ∑j=07 C(j + 3, j) by parallel summation.
  • 6.
    Evaluate ∑ₖ C(8, k) C(5, 6 − k).
  • 7.
    Find the coefficient of x⁵ in (1 − 2x)⁻³.
  • 8.
    Find the coefficient of x⁴ in √(1 + x).
  • 9.
    Evaluate ∑ₖ C(12 − k, k).
  • 10.
    Write x³ in the basis C(x, k) and use it to evaluate ∑j=09 j³.
  • 11.
    Compute C(1000, 300) mod 7 by Lucas' theorem.
  • 12.
    How many odd entries does row 1000 of Pascal's triangle contain?
  • 13.
    Compute v₂(100!) and v₃(100!) in two ways.
  • 14.
    Compute v₂ C(200, 100) and v₃ C(200, 100).
  • 15.
    Find the coefficient of x²y²z² in (x + y + z)⁶.
  • 16.
    Find the coefficient of x²yz³ in (3x + y − 2z)⁶.
  • 17.
    Expand the Gaussian coefficient [4 2] as a polynomial in q, and evaluate it at q = 2 and at q = −1.
  • 18.
    Compute C₈ and state what it counts for convex polygons.
  • 19.
    In an election A receives 9 votes and B receives 5. Find the probability that A is strictly ahead throughout a random count.
  • 20.
    Estimate C(50, 25) using 4ⁿ/√(πn) with and without the correction factor 1 − 1/(8n), and compare with the exact value 126410606437752.

Advanced (Problems 21–45)

  • 21.
    Prove that (1 − x)⁻ᵐ = ∑ₖ C(m + k − 1, k) xᵏ as formal power series, and find the coefficient of x⁷ in (1 − x)⁻⁴.
  • 22.
    Prove ∑ₖ C(n, k) C(k, m) = C(n, m) 2ⁿ⁻ᵐ and evaluate it for n = 10, m = 3.
  • 23.
    Prove ∑ₖ (−1)ᵏ C(n, k) C(k, m) = (−1)ᵐ if n = m and 0 otherwise. Evaluate the sum for n = 7, m = 3.
  • 24.
    Prove ∑k=0n C(2k, k) C(2n − 2k, n − k) = 4ⁿ and verify it for n = 4.
  • 25.
    Evaluate ∑k=0n C(n, k)/(k + 1) in closed form, and compute it for n = 9.
  • 26.
    Compute the number of derangements D₆ by binomial inversion.
  • 27.
    Compute the determinant of the symmetric Pascal matrix with entries C(i + j, i), 0 ≤ i, j ≤ 3, without expanding it.
  • 28.
    Determine whether ∑ₖ C(−1/3, k) xᵏ converges at x = 1 and at x = −1, and give the sum when it converges.
  • 29.
    Approximate √1.2 by the first four terms of the binomial series and bound the error.
  • 30.
    Find the coefficients of x⁶ in (1 − 4x)−1/2 and in (1 − 4x)1/2.
  • 31.
    Show that (x³ − x)/6 is integer-valued by writing it in the binomial basis, and identify it as a single binomial coefficient.
  • 32.
    Find the polynomial f of degree at most 3 with f(0) = 2, f(1) = 5, f(2) = 14, f(3) = 35, in the binomial basis and in the monomial basis, and compute f(4).
  • 33.
    Evaluate ∑k=05 (−1)ᵏ C(5, k) k⁵.
  • 34.
    Show that no entry of row 242 is divisible by 3.
  • 35.
    Determine all n ≥ 2 such that C(n, k) is divisible by 6 for every 0 < k < n.
  • 36.
    How many entries of row 2026 are divisible by 5?
  • 37.
    Find the smallest n such that 2⁵ divides C(2n, n).
  • 38.
    Verify Babbage's congruence for p = 5, and show that in this case even the congruence modulo 5³ holds.
  • 39.
    Verify Wolstenholme's congruence C(2p, p) ≡ 2 (mod p³) for p = 11.
  • 40.
    Using Corollary 8.3, explain why C(10, 4) divides lcm(1, …, 10), and compute the quotient.
  • 41.
    Compute the number of odd entries in rows 0 through 99 of Pascal's triangle.
  • 42.
    Compute the Hausdorff dimension of the limiting fractal of Pascal's triangle modulo 3.
  • 43.
    Find the largest coefficient of (x + y + z + w)⁹.
  • 44.
    How many 2-dimensional subspaces does F₅⁴ have?
  • 45.
    Count the lattice paths from (0, 0) to (9, 4) with unit steps east and north that never go above the line y = x.

Challenge (Problems 46–60)

  • 46.
    Use Chu–Vandermonde with α = 1/2 and β = −1/2 to prove ∑k=0n C(2k, k) C(2n − 2k, n − k)/(2k − 1) = 0 for n ≥ 1. Verify it for n = 2.
  • 47.
    Verify Dixon's identity ∑ₖ (−1)ᵏ C(2n, k)³ = (−1)ⁿ (3n)!/(n!)³ for n = 2.
  • 48.
    Compute v₂(C₁₀₀), where C₁₀₀ is the 100th Catalan number.
  • 49.
    Show without computing C(20, 10) that 7 does not divide it, then confirm with the factorization of C(20, 10).
  • 50.
    Prove that ∏p≤m p < 4ᵐ for all m ≥ 1, and check it for m = 30.
  • 51.
    Verify that the Fibonacci family of Theorem 14.2 with i = 2 satisfies the Diophantine condition, and list the six positions of the value a = C(104, 39).
  • 52.
    List all occurrences of 3003 in Pascal's triangle and compare with the bound of Proposition 14.1.
  • 53.
    Find the coefficient of x² in ∏i=03 (1 + qⁱx), both directly and by the q-binomial theorem.
  • 54.
    Verify Gauss's evaluation ∑ₖ (−1)ᵏ [6 k] = (1 − q)(1 − q³)(1 − q⁵) at q = 2 by computing all seven Gaussian coefficients [6 k]₂.
  • 55.
    Compare ∑k≤25 C(100, k) with the Hamming-ball bound 2100 H(1/4).
  • 56.
    Compute the probability that a simple random walk is at 0 at time 200, and compare with 1/√(100π) and its corrected version.
  • 57.
    Compute v₂ of the multinomial coefficient C(15; 5, 5, 5) by Theorem 10.2 and confirm it from the value of the coefficient.
  • 58.
    Check the bounds of Proposition 13.1 for n = 10.
  • 59.
    Find the smallest n such that row n contains exactly 12 entries not divisible by 3.
  • 60.
    Prove ∑k=0n (−1)ᵏ C(n, k)/(x + k) = n!/(x(x + 1)⋯(x + n)) and evaluate the left side for n = 4, x = 1.
