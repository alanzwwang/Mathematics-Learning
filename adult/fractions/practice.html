Problem Set

60 problems

Overview123456789101112ProblemsAssessment

The problems are arranged in three groups. The core problems test the definitions and computational techniques of the lessons. The advanced problems ask for short proofs and longer computations. The challenge problems are substantial: several of them are classical theorems whose proofs require combining ideas from different lessons.

Core (Problems 1–20)

  • 1.
    In ℤ/6ℤ, exhibit pairs showing that the relation (a, b) ~ (c, d) ⟺ ad = bc on ℤ/6ℤ × (ℤ/6ℤ ∖ {0}) is not transitive.
  • 2.
    Write x/(x + 1) + 1/(x − 1) as a single reduced element of ℚ(x).
  • 3.
    Compute vp(−540/77) for every prime p.
  • 4.
    Use the rational root theorem to prove that √12 is irrational.
  • 5.
    Find all rational roots of 6x³ − 11x² + 6x − 1.
  • 6.
    Write 0.3272727… (the block 27 repeating) as a reduced fraction.
  • 7.
    Find the periods of the decimal expansions of 1/13, 1/17 and 1/37.
  • 8.
    Find the preperiod and period of 7/120 without dividing, then confirm by computing the expansion.
  • 9.
    Write out the Farey sequence F6.
  • 10.
    Compute |F10|.
  • 11.
    Find the neighbours of 5/12 in F12.
  • 12.
    Decide whether the Ford circles are tangent or disjoint: (i) C(5/13) and C(3/8); (ii) C(1/3) and C(3/7).
  • 13.
    Find the continued fraction of 415/93 and all its convergents.
  • 14.
    Evaluate [1; 2, 3, 4] as a reduced fraction.
  • 15.
    Find the continued fraction of √3 and the fundamental solution of x² − 3y² = 1.
  • 16.
    Decompose 1/(x² − 4) into partial fractions over ℚ.
  • 17.
    Decompose (2x² + 3)/(x(x² + 1)) into partial fractions over ℝ.
  • 18.
    Apply the greedy algorithm to 5/6 and to 7/15.
  • 19.
    Compute |1/48|2, |48|2, |−7/9|3 and |1000/27|5.
  • 20.
    Compute |3/8|2, |5/8|2 and |3/8 + 5/8|2, and explain how the result illustrates the ultrametric inequality.

Advanced (Problems 21–45)

  • 21.
    Prove that every ring homomorphism from ℚ to a field K is injective, and that there is at most one. Deduce that Aut(ℚ) is trivial.
  • 22.
    Show directly that every quotient of Gaussian integers lies in ℚ(i), and compute (2 + 3i)/(4 − i).
  • 23.
    Prove that ℤ₍ₚ₎ = {a/b ∈ ℚ : p ∤ b} is a local ring with maximal ideal pℤ₍ₚ₎.
  • 24.
    Prove that Hn = 1 + 1/2 + ⋯ + 1/n is not an integer for n ≥ 2.
  • 25.
    Prove that v2(Hn) = −⌊log₂ n⌋ for n ≥ 1, and compute v2(H20).
  • 26.
    Find the period of 1/49, and explain why it equals 7 times the period of 1/7.
  • 27.
    Find the period of 1/91 and write out the repeating block.
  • 28.
    List the primes p < 50 with p ≠ 2, 5 for which 1/p has period p − 1.
  • 29.
    Verify Midy's theorem for 1/17, whose period is 16.
  • 30.
    Show that 1/4 = 0.(02) in base 3, and determine the base-3 expansion of 1/2.
  • 31.
    Given the consecutive terms 2/7 < 1/3 of F7, compute the next term using the successor formula.
  • 32.
    Prove that b + d > n for consecutive terms a/b < c/d of Fn, and that b ≠ d when n ≥ 2.
  • 33.
    Find the Stern–Brocot path of 7/5 and of 13/8, and relate them to the continued fractions.
  • 34.
    Evaluate the total area of the Ford circles with base points in [0, 1) to four decimal places, given ζ(3) ≈ 1.2020569 and ζ(4) = π⁴/90.
  • 35.
    Compute the convergents of e = [2; 1, 2, 1, 1, 4, 1, 1, …] up to 193/71, and estimate e − 87/32.
  • 36.
    Use Theorem 7.2 to bound |π − 355/113| from above and below, given q₄ = 33102 and q₃ = 113.
  • 37.
    Is 13/8 a convergent of φ? Check Legendre's criterion for it directly.
  • 38.
    Find the first three positive solutions of x² − 2y² = 1.
  • 39.
    Find the first three positive solutions of x² − 2y² = −1.
  • 40.
    Find the continued fraction of √23 and the fundamental solution of x² − 23y² = 1.
  • 41.
    Prove that √2 + √3 is irrational.
  • 42.
    Prove that ∑n ≥ 1 2−n² is irrational.
  • 43.
    Using Dirichlet's theorem with N = 7, find q ≤ 7 and p with |qπ − p| < 1/7.
  • 44.
    Evaluate ∑n ≥ 1 1/(n(n + 1)(n + 2)).
  • 45.
    Use partial fractions to find a closed form for the coefficients of 1/((1 − x)(1 − 2x)).

Challenge (Problems 46–60)

  • 46.
    Let D be a unique factorisation domain. Prove that every element of Frac(D) has a reduced representation a/b (gcd(a, b) = 1), unique up to multiplying a and b by the same unit.
  • 47.
    Prove that Frac(K[[x]]) = K((x)) for a field K.
  • 48.
    Prove that a purely periodic continued fraction represents a quadratic irrational, and evaluate the purely periodic expansion with repeating block 1, 2.
  • 49.
    Prove that qn²|φ − pn/qn| → 1/√5 for the convergents of the golden ratio, and deduce that √5 in Hurwitz's theorem cannot be replaced by a larger constant.
  • 50.
    The continued fraction of √61 has period 11 and p₁₀ = 29718, q₁₀ = 3805. Find the fundamental solution of x² − 61y² = 1.
  • 51.
    Prove that the Liouville number L = ∑k ≥ 1 10−k! is transcendental.
  • 52.
    Prove that log₁₀ 2 is irrational, deduce that some power of 2 has leading digit 7, and find the least such power.
  • 53.
    Prove that it suffices to prove the Erdős–Straus conjecture for primes, and prove it for all n ≡ 3 (mod 4) and all n ≡ 2 (mod 3).
  • 54.
    Verify the greedy expansion of 5/121 given in Lesson 11 for its first two steps, and verify that 5/121 = 1/33 + 1/121 + 1/363.
  • 55.
    For Sylvester's sequence s₁ = 2, sn+1 = sn² − sn + 1, prove that ∑k=1n 1/sk = 1 − 1/(sn+1 − 1) and that the sk are pairwise coprime.
  • 56.
    Prove the non-archimedean case of Ostrowski's theorem.
  • 57.
    Find the 2-adic expansion of 1/5 and verify that it is eventually periodic.
  • 58.
    Use Hensel's lemma to find a 5-adic integer α with α² = −1 and α ≡ 2 (mod 5), to three digits.
  • 59.
    Prove the product formula for every x ∈ ℚ×.
  • 60.
    Let N ≥ 2 be a non-square and let (x₁, y₁) be the smallest positive solution of x² − Ny² = 1, with ε = x₁ + y₁√N. Prove that the positive solutions are exactly the (xk, yk) defined by xk + yk√N = εk, k ≥ 1.
