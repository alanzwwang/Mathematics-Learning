Similarity Dimension, the Moran Equation and the Open Set Condition

Lesson 4 of 13

Overview12345678910111213ProblemsAssessment

Key idea of this section

For an IFS of similarities with ratios r₁, …, rN, the similarity dimension s is
the unique solution of   r₁ˢ + r₂ˢ + ⋯ + rNˢ = 1   (Moran's equation).

The Moran equation

Let {f₁, …, fN} be similarities of ℝⁿ with ratios 0 < ri < 1. The function φ(t) = ∑ riᵗ is continuous and strictly decreasing, with φ(0) = N ≥ 2 and φ(t) → 0 as t → ∞. Hence there is a unique s > 0 with φ(s) = 1; it is called the similarity dimension of the IFS. When all ratios are equal to r, Moran's equation reads N rˢ = 1 and gives the familiar formula

s = log N / log(1/r)

The heuristic is scaling: if F = ⋃ fi(F) with the pieces essentially disjoint, and if some s-dimensional "size" 𝓗ˢ scales by riˢ under fi, then 𝓗ˢ(F) = ∑ riˢ 𝓗ˢ(F), and a size that is neither 0 nor ∞ requires ∑ riˢ = 1. Lesson 8 turns this heuristic into a theorem.

Worked example 1 (two different ratios). Take f₁(x) = x/2 and f₂(x) = x/4 + 3/4 on ℝ, whose attractor is a Cantor set with first-level pieces [0, 1/2] and [3/4, 1]. Setting u = 2−s, Moran's equation reads u + u² = 1, so u = (√5 − 1)/2 = 1/φ, the reciprocal of the golden ratio. Hence

s = log φ / log 2 = 0.4812 / 0.6931 ≈ 0.6942

Worked example 2. Take ratios 1/2, 1/4, 1/4. With u = 2−s, the equation u + 2u² = 1 has positive root u = 1/2, so s = 1 exactly. (Indeed [0, 1] is the attractor of x ↦ x/2, x ↦ x/4 + 1/2, x ↦ x/4 + 3/4.)

Similarity dimension is a property of the IFS, not of the set

The same set may be generated by many IFSs. [0, 1] is generated by k maps of ratio 1/k for every k ≥ 2, always with s = 1. But [0, 1] is also the attractor of the three maps x ↦ x/2, x ↦ x/2 + 1/2 and x ↦ x/2 + 1/4, whose similarity dimension is log 3 / log 2 ≈ 1.585 > 1. Overlaps inflate the similarity dimension. In general the Hausdorff dimension of the attractor (Lesson 6) satisfies dimH F ≤ min{s, n}, and equality can fail badly when pieces overlap.

The open set condition

Definition. The IFS satisfies the open set condition (OSC) if there is a non-empty bounded open set V ⊂ ℝⁿ with

fi(V) ⊂ V for every i,  and  fi(V) ∩ fj(V) = ∅ for i ≠ j

The OSC formalises "the pieces fi(F) touch, if at all, only in a negligible way". For the Cantor set take V = (0, 1). For the Sierpinski triangle take V to be the open triangle: the three half-size open triangles are disjoint although their closures meet at three points. For the Koch curve one can take V to be the interior of the isosceles triangle with base [0, 1] and apex at the peak of the curve.

The OSC is stronger than requiring the attractor's pieces to be disjoint up to measure zero, and weaker than the strong separation condition fi(F) ∩ fj(F) = ∅. A theorem of Schief (1994) shows that for similarities the OSC is equivalent to the strong open set condition (one may take V ∩ F ≠ ∅) and to 0 < 𝓗ˢ(F), where s is the similarity dimension.

Theorem 4.1 (Moran 1946, Hutchinson 1981). If an IFS of similarities on ℝⁿ satisfies the open set condition, then the attractor F satisfies

dimH F = dimB F = s,  and  0 < 𝓗ˢ(F) < ∞

The proof requires the Hausdorff measure, defined in Lesson 6; we give it in Lesson 8.

Dimension of products and graph-directed sets

If F ⊂ ℝ is the attractor of N₁ similarities of ratio r and E ⊂ ℝ of N₂ similarities of the same ratio r, then F × E ⊂ ℝ² is the attractor of N₁N₂ similarities of ratio r, so its similarity dimension is log(N₁N₂)/log(1/r) = sF + sE. For example C × C, the Cantor dust, has dimension 2 log 2 / log 3 = log 4 / log 3 ≈ 1.2619 — the same as the Koch curve, though one is totally disconnected and the other is an arc. Dimension does not determine topology.

More general self-similar structures arise when the pieces of several sets are copies of one another (graph-directed constructions of Mauldin and Williams, 1988). The dimension is then the unique s for which the matrix M(s) with entries ∑ reˢ over edges e from vertex i to vertex j has spectral radius 1, which reduces to Moran's equation when there is a single vertex.

Exercise

  • (a)
    Find the similarity dimension of the Sierpinski carpet (keep 8 of the 9 subsquares of side 1/3) and of the Menger sponge (keep 20 of the 27 subcubes).
  • (b)
    Solve Moran's equation for ratios 1/3, 1/3, 1/9.
  • (c)
    Show that the similarity dimension of the IFS with ratios r₁, …, rN is at most n whenever the OSC holds in ℝⁿ.
  • (d)
    Find the dimension of the set of x ∈ [0, 1] whose base-5 expansion uses only the digits 0, 2 and 4.
Show solution

Answers: (a) log 8 / log 3 ≈ 1.8928 and log 20 / log 3 ≈ 2.7268. (b) With u = 3−s: 2u + u² = 1, so u = √2 − 1 and s = −log(√2 − 1)/log 3 = log(1 + √2)/log 3 ≈ 0.8814/1.0986 ≈ 0.8023. (c) The sets fi(V) are disjoint subsets of V with volumes riⁿ vol(V), so ∑ riⁿ ≤ 1 = ∑ riˢ; since t ↦ ∑ riᵗ is decreasing, s ≤ n. (d) Three maps of ratio 1/5 with disjoint pieces [0, 1/5], [2/5, 3/5], [4/5, 1]: s = log 3 / log 5 ≈ 0.6826.
