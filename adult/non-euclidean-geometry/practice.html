Problem Set

60 problems

Overview12345678910111213ProblemsAssessment

The problems are grouped by difficulty. Core problems check fluency with the basic formulas of each model; Advanced problems require combining several results; Challenge problems ask for proofs or for connections between lessons. Unless stated otherwise, the sphere has radius 1, the hyperbolic plane has curvature −1, and angles are in radians.

Core (Problems 1–20)

  • 1.
    Find the area of a spherical triangle with angles 90°, 60°, 60°.
  • 2.
    On a sphere of radius 2, find the area of a spherical triangle with three angles of 120°.
  • 3.
    Find the area of a lune of angle π/5 on a sphere of radius 3.
  • 4.
    A right spherical triangle has legs a = b = π/4. Find its hypotenuse.
  • 5.
    Find the great-circle distance between the points with (latitude, longitude) (0°, 0°) and (45°N, 90°E) on the unit sphere.
  • 6.
    Verify that (3, 0, √10) lies on the hyperboloid model H² and find its distance from (0, 0, 1).
  • 7.
    Find d(i, 4i) in the upper half-plane.
  • 8.
    Find d(1 + i, 4 + 5i) in the upper half-plane.
  • 9.
    Classify the isometry given by (4 3; 1 1) and find its translation length.
  • 10.
    Classify (0 −1; 1 1), find its fixed point in H and its order in PSL(2,ℝ).
  • 11.
    Classify (1 4; 0 1) and describe its action.
  • 12.
    Find the image of i under g = (2 1; 1 1) and check the formula Im g(z) = Im z/|cz + d|².
  • 13.
    In the Poincaré disk, find d(0, 3/5).
  • 14.
    Find the Klein-model coordinate of the Poincaré-disk point 3/5.
  • 15.
    Find the area of a hyperbolic triangle with angles 20°, 30° and 40°.
  • 16.
    Find the area of a hyperbolic pentagon all of whose angles are right angles.
  • 17.
    A right hyperbolic triangle has legs with cosh a = 2, cosh b = 5. Find cosh of the hypotenuse.
  • 18.
    Find the angle of parallelism Π(ln 5).
  • 19.
    Find the circumference and area of a hyperbolic circle of radius ln 3.
  • 20.
    Find the Gaussian curvature of ds² = 9(dx² + dy²)/y².

Advanced (Problems 21–45)

  • 21.
    Prove in neutral geometry that the summit angles of a Saccheri quadrilateral are equal.
  • 22.
    Show in neutral geometry that the summit of a Saccheri quadrilateral is at least as long as its base.
  • 23.
    Find the angles and area of the equilateral spherical triangle with sides π/3.
  • 24.
    Find the sides of the spherical triangle with angles π/2, π/2, π/3.
  • 25.
    Estimate the great-circle distance between New York (40.71°N, 74.01°W) and London (51.51°N, 0.13°W), with R = 6371 km.
  • 26.
    Find d(i, 1 + 2i) and express it in terms of the golden ratio.
  • 27.
    Find the geodesic through i and 2 + 2i and its ideal endpoints.
  • 28.
    Find an element of PSL(2,ℝ) mapping the geodesic from 0 to ∞ onto the unit semicircle, with 0 ↦ −1 and ∞ ↦ 1.
  • 29.
    Find the translation length, the fixed points and the axis of (3 1; 2 1).
  • 30.
    Show that an elliptic element of order n in PSL(2,ℝ) (rotation by 2π/n) has |tr| = 2cos(π/n), and deduce the possible finite orders of elements of PSL(2,ℤ).
  • 31.
    Find the distance between 1/2 and i/2 in the Poincaré disk.
  • 32.
    Compute the Cayley transform of 2i and verify that it is consistent with d(i, 2i) = ln 2.
  • 33.
    In the Beltrami–Klein model, find d(0, 1/2).
  • 34.
    A hyperbolic triangle has cosh a = 2, cosh b = 3 and γ = π/3. Find c.
  • 35.
    Find the sides of the hyperbolic triangle with angles π/3, π/4, π/4, and its area.
  • 36.
    Find the area and circumradius of the regular right-angled hyperbolic hexagon.
  • 37.
    Find the area and side length of a tile of the {3,7} tiling.
  • 38.
    Which of the Schläfli symbols {4,4}, {5,4}, {3,6}, {3,7}, {6,3}, {4,5}, {3,5} define hyperbolic tilings?
  • 39.
    Find the covolume of Δ(2,3,8), and deduce an upper bound for the number of orientation-preserving symmetries of a hyperbolic surface of genus 2 whose symmetry group is a quotient of Δ(2,3,8).
  • 40.
    The congruence subgroup Γ₀(p) has index p + 1 in PSL(2,ℤ) for p prime. Find the area of H/Γ₀(5).
  • 41.
    Find the Gaussian curvature of ds² = dx² + e2x dy².
  • 42.
    The torus of revolution with radii R > r has K = cos v / (r(R + r cos v)). For R = 3, r = 1, find K on the outer and inner equators and verify ∫∫ K dA = 0.
  • 43.
    A geodesic triangle of area 0.3 lies on a surface with −2 ≤ K ≤ −1. Bound its angle sum.
  • 44.
    Find the holonomy of parallel transport around the parallel of latitude 30° on the unit sphere, and the corresponding Foucault rotation per sidereal day.
  • 45.
    Find the Christoffel symbols of dφ² + sin²φ dλ² and show that a circle of latitude φ = φ₀ is a geodesic only if φ₀ = π/2.

Challenge (Problems 46–60)

  • 46.
    Show that a small equilateral spherical triangle of side s has excess √3 s²/4 + O(s⁴), so that Girard's theorem reduces to the Euclidean area formula.
  • 47.
    Prove that every hyperbolic triangle has inradius at most ½ ln 3.
  • 48.
    Let A = (1 1; 1 2) and B = (1 −1; −1 2). Compute tr A, tr B, tr AB and tr[A, B] using the Fricke identity, and interpret the result.
  • 49.
    Use the Hurwitz bound to find the maximum order of the automorphism group of a closed Riemann surface of genus 7, and name a surface attaining it.
  • 50.
    Compute the area of the standard fundamental domain of PSL(2,ℤ) by direct integration.
  • 51.
    Prove that for g(z) = λz, λ > 1, the minimum of d(z, gz) is ln λ, attained exactly on the imaginary axis.
  • 52.
    For a closed hyperbolic surface of genus 2, estimate the number of orbit points γ(p) within distance 10 of p.
  • 53.
    Compute the total curvature of the catenoid x(u, v) = (cosh v cos u, cosh v sin u, v).
  • 54.
    Verify Gauss–Bonnet with boundary for a geodesic disk of radius r on the unit sphere and on the hyperbolic plane.
  • 55.
    Show that a horocycle has geodesic curvature 1, and verify Gauss–Bonnet for the cusp region {Im z ≥ 1}/(z ↦ z + 2π).
  • 56.
    Find the regular hyperbolic tiling {p, q} whose tiles have the smallest possible area.
  • 57.
    Show that the three points −1 + i, i, 1 + i in H do not lie on any hyperbolic circle, and explain which equivalent of the parallel postulate this violates.
  • 58.
    In the hyperboloid model, show that the lines with unit normals n₁ = (1, 0, 0) and n₂ = (cosh 2, 0, sinh 2) are ultraparallel and find the length of their common perpendicular.
  • 59.
    How many tiles of the {8,3} tiling form a fundamental domain of a genus-2 surface? Check with the Euler characteristic.
  • 60.
    In an open FLRW universe whose spatial sections are hyperbolic with curvature radius Rc, find the area of a sphere of geodesic radius r, and its ratio to the Euclidean value at r = Rc.
