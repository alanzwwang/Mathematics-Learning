Problem Set

60 problems

Overview123456789101112ProblemsAssessment

The problems are arranged in three groups. The Core problems check fluency with the definitions and standard computations of Lessons 1 to 12. The Advanced problems require combining several ideas or writing a short proof. The Challenge problems are of the kind found on graduate qualifying examinations; several of them contain classical results in disguise. Give exact answers where possible and decimals to three or four significant figures otherwise.

Core (Problems 1–20)

  • 1.
    A σ-algebra on Ω is generated by a partition of Ω into 5 nonempty sets. How many events does it contain?
  • 2.
    Let 𝓐 be the collection of subsets A ⊆ ℕ such that A or ℕ \ A is finite. Show that 𝓐 is an algebra but not a σ-algebra.
  • 3.
    Events satisfy P(A) = 0.5, P(B) = 0.4 and P(A ∪ B) = 0.7. Find P(A ∩ B), P(A ∩ Bᶜ) and P(A | B). Are A and B independent?
  • 4.
    Three fair dice are rolled. Find the probability that the total is 10.
  • 5.
    Machines I, II and III produce 50%, 30% and 20% of a factory's output, with defect rates 1%, 2% and 3%. Find the probability that a random item is defective, and the probability that a defective item came from machine III.
  • 6.
    Find the probability that a uniformly random permutation of {1, 2, 3, 4, 5} has no fixed point.
  • 7.
    Let X ~ Poisson(2). Compute P(X ≥ 2).
  • 8.
    A random variable has density f(x) = cx² on (0, 2). Find c, E X and Var X.
  • 9.
    Let Z ~ N(0, 1) and Y = eZ. Find the density of Y and E Y.
  • 10.
    Let X₁ ~ Exponential(λ₁) and X₂ ~ Exponential(λ₂) be independent. Find the law of min(X₁, X₂) and P(X₁ < X₂).
  • 11.
    Compute the probability generating function of X ~ Geometric(p) on {1, 2, …} and use it to find E X and Var X.
  • 12.
    A fair die is rolled 6 times. Find the expected number of distinct faces that appear.
  • 13.
    A nonnegative random variable has E X = 2. Bound P(X ≥ 8). If also Var X = 1, give a better bound.
  • 14.
    Use Jensen's inequality to show E log X ≤ log E X for positive integrable X, and check it when X is uniform on {1, 4}.
  • 15.
    Show that Cov(X + Y, X − Y) = Var X − Var Y for X, Y ∈ L².
  • 16.
    Find the characteristic function of Binomial(n, p).
  • 17.
    Using the MGF of the standard normal, compute E Z⁴.
  • 18.
    Find the stationary distribution of the two-state chain with p(1, 2) = 0.2 and p(2, 1) = 0.3.
  • 19.
    For a Poisson process of rate 2, compute P(N(3) = 5 | N(1) = 2).
  • 20.
    A fair random walk starts at 4 and is absorbed at 0 or 12. Find the probability of absorption at 12 and the expected absorption time.

Advanced (Problems 21–45)

  • 21.
    Show that continuity from above can fail for a measure of infinite total mass, by an example on ℕ with counting measure. Why does this not contradict Theorem 1.3?
  • 22.
    Prove that if X ≥ 0 and E X = 0, then X = 0 almost surely.
  • 23.
    Let X be a random variable that is independent of itself. Prove that X is almost surely constant.
  • 24.
    Let U ~ Uniform(0, 1). Find the law of Y = −2 log U and its mean.
  • 25.
    Let X, Y be i.i.d. Exponential(1). Show that X/(X + Y) is Uniform(0, 1) and independent of X + Y.
  • 26.
    Let X, Y be i.i.d. N(0, 1). Show that X/Y has the standard Cauchy law.
  • 27.
    Compute E|Z| for Z ~ N(0, 1).
  • 28.
    Compute E max(X, Y) for i.i.d. standard normals X, Y.
  • 29.
    Compute the variance of the coupon collector time for n = 6 coupon types.
  • 30.
    A branching process has Poisson(2) offspring. Find its extinction probability numerically to four decimal places.
  • 31.
    Use the Chernoff method to prove P(X ≥ a) ≤ e−λ(eλ/a)a for X ~ Poisson(λ) and a > λ, and evaluate the bound for λ = 10, a = 20.
  • 32.
    Prove the Paley–Zygmund inequality P(X > θ E X) ≥ (1 − θ)² (E X)²/E X² for X ≥ 0 in L² and θ ∈ [0, 1]. Evaluate it when E X = 1, E X² = 4 and θ = 1/2.
  • 33.
    Show that d(X, Y) = E min(|X − Y|, 1) metrises convergence in probability.
  • 34.
    Let Xₙ ~ N(0, 1/n). Show that Xₙ → 0 in probability. Does Xₙ → 0 almost surely, whatever the joint law of the sequence?
  • 35.
    Let U₁, U₂, … be i.i.d. Uniform(0, 1). Find the almost sure limit of (U₁U₂⋯Uₙ)1/n.
  • 36.
    Let Mₙ be the maximum of n i.i.d. Exponential(1) variables. Show that Mₙ − log n converges in distribution and identify the limit.
  • 37.
    Let mₙ be the minimum of n i.i.d. Uniform(0, 1) variables. Find the limit in distribution of n mₙ.
  • 38.
    Let X ~ Poisson(100). Use the normal approximation with continuity correction to estimate P(X ≤ 90).
  • 39.
    How large a sample is needed so that a 95% normal-approximation confidence interval for an unknown proportion has half-width at most 0.03, whatever the true proportion?
  • 40.
    Let X̄ₙ be the mean of n i.i.d. Bernoulli(p) variables. Find the asymptotic variance of √n(X̄ₙ(1 − X̄ₙ) − p(1 − p)), and evaluate it at p = 0.3.
  • 41.
    Let X, Y be i.i.d. integrable. Show that E[X | X + Y] = (X + Y)/2.
  • 42.
    Let X ~ Uniform(0, 1) and, given X, let Y ~ Uniform(0, X). Find E Y and Var Y.
  • 43.
    Let N ~ Poisson(4) and let X₁, X₂, … be i.i.d. Exponential with mean 2, independent of N. Find the mean and variance of S = X₁ + ⋯ + XN.
  • 44.
    A gambler with 10 units bets one unit at a time at odds p = 0.45 of winning, until reaching 0 or 20. Find the probability of reaching 20.
  • 45.
    A random walk moves to a uniformly chosen neighbour on the 3 × 3 grid graph. Find the mean return time to a corner vertex and to the centre.

Challenge (Problems 46–60)

  • 46.
    Show that there is no probability measure on all subsets of ℕ giving every singleton the same probability. Is there a finitely additive one?
  • 47.
    Let Sₙ be the simple symmetric random walk on ℤ. Prove that lim sup Sₙ/√n = +∞ almost surely.
  • 48.
    Prove Kolmogorov's maximal inequality: for independent mean-zero Xᵢ ∈ L², P(maxk≤n |Sₖ| ≥ λ) ≤ Var Sₙ/λ².
  • 49.
    Prove that E|X| < ∞ if and only if ∑n≥1 P(|X| ≥ n) < ∞.
  • 50.
    Compute the characteristic function of the Laplace density ½e−|x|, and use the inversion formula to deduce the characteristic function of the standard Cauchy law.
  • 51.
    Let U₁, U₂, … be i.i.d. Uniform(0, 1) and N = min{n : U₁ + ⋯ + Uₙ > 1}. Find E N.
  • 52.
    In an election candidate A receives 6 votes and candidate B receives 4, counted in uniformly random order. Find the probability that A is strictly ahead throughout the count.
  • 53.
    In Pólya's urn (start with one red and one blue ball; each drawn ball is returned with another of its colour), show that after n draws the number of red balls added is uniform on {0, 1, …, n}, and deduce the law of the limiting fraction.
  • 54.
    For the biased gambler's ruin with p = 0.4, starting at 5 with barriers 0 and 10, compute the expected duration using the martingale Sₙ − n(p − q).
  • 55.
    A fair coin is tossed repeatedly. Find the expected number of tosses until the pattern HH first appears, and until HT first appears.
  • 56.
    A walk on {0, 1, …, n} moves from 0 to 1 with probability 1 and from each other interior point to either neighbour with probability 1/2. Find the expected time to reach n from 0.
  • 57.
    Let X₁, X₂, … be i.i.d. with E|X₁| = ∞. Prove that lim sup |Sₙ|/n = ∞ almost surely.
  • 58.
    For a Poisson process of rate λ, compute E ∑i : Sᵢ ≤ t Sᵢ, the expected sum of the arrival times in [0, t]. Evaluate it for λ = 3, t = 2.
  • 59.
    Compare Binomial(100, 0.02) with Poisson(2): give Le Cam's bound on ∑ₖ |P(X = k) − P(Y = k)| and compare the two probabilities of zero.
  • 60.
    Prove that e−n ∑k=0n nᵏ/k! → 1/2 as n → ∞.
