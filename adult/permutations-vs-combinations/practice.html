Problem Set

60 problems

Overview12345678910111213ProblemsAssessment

The problems are arranged in three groups. The core problems test the definitions and the basic computations of every lesson. The advanced problems require combining several ideas or writing a short proof. The challenge problems are proofs and computations of the kind found on qualifying examinations in algebra and combinatorics. Composition of permutations is always right to left, (στ)(i) = σ(τ(i)).

Core (Problems 1–20)

  • 1.
    How many injections are there from a 5-element set into a 9-element set?
  • 2.
    Write σ = 5 3 1 2 4 7 6 (one-line notation) in disjoint cycle notation and find its order.
  • 3.
    Compute the product (1 3 5)(1 2) in S₅.
  • 4.
    Find the inverse of (1 4 2 6)(3 5) in S₆.
  • 5.
    Find the number of inversions and the sign of σ = 4 2 5 1 3, and confirm the sign from the cycle decomposition.
  • 6.
    Write (1 2 3 4 5) as a product of the fewest possible transpositions. How many are needed?
  • 7.
    What is the order of (1 2 3)(4 5 6 7)(8 9)?
  • 8.
    How many elements of S₇ have the same cycle type as (1 2)(3 4 5)?
  • 9.
    Show that (1 2 3)(4 5) and (1 3 4)(2 5) are conjugate in S₅ by finding an explicit conjugating permutation.
  • 10.
    List the unsigned Stirling numbers c(5, k) for k = 1, …, 5 and check that they sum to 5!.
  • 11.
    Compute the number of derangements D₆.
  • 12.
    What is the probability that a uniformly random permutation of [6] has exactly two fixed points?
  • 13.
    How many distinct arrangements of the letters of STATISTICS are there?
  • 14.
    How many multisets of size 5 can be formed from 7 types of objects?
  • 15.
    How many solutions in nonnegative integers does x₁ + x₂ + x₃ + x₄ = 15 have with x₁ ≥ 3 and x₂ ≥ 1?
  • 16.
    Compute S(6, 2), the number of ways to split 6 labelled people into 2 nonempty unlabelled groups.
  • 17.
    Find the lexicographic rank (counting from 0) of 2 4 1 3 in S₄.
  • 18.
    Write the 4-bit binary reflected Gray code word with index 11.
  • 19.
    How many 2-colour necklaces with 4 beads are there up to rotation?
  • 20.
    Find the expected number of cycles of a uniformly random permutation of [6].

Advanced (Problems 21–45)

  • 21.
    Prove that every product of two transpositions is a product of 3-cycles, and deduce that Aₙ is generated by 3-cycles for n ≥ 3.
  • 22.
    How many elements of order exactly 2 does S₆ contain?
  • 23.
    How many elements of order exactly 6 does S₆ contain?
  • 24.
    What is the largest order of an element of A₈?
  • 25.
    Find the sign of σ = 2 3 4 5 1 7 6 9 8 in S₉ using the number of cycles of even length.
  • 26.
    Compute zλ and the class size in S₈ for cycle type λ = (2, 2, 2, 1, 1).
  • 27.
    Verify the identity ∑λ⊢4 1/zλ = 1 by listing the five cycle types of S₄.
  • 28.
    Prove that c(n, 2) = (n − 1)! Hₙ₋₁ and use it to compute c(7, 2).
  • 29.
    Prove that ∑ₖ (−1)ᵏ c(n, k) = 0 for n ≥ 2, and interpret the identity.
  • 30.
    Compute Foata's transformation Φ((1 5 2)(3)(4 7 6)) and verify that the number of records equals the number of cycles.
  • 31.
    Prove Dₙ = nDₙ₋₁ + (−1)ⁿ directly from the inclusion–exclusion formula, and compute D₁₀.
  • 32.
    How many permutations of [7] have neither fixed points nor 2-cycles?
  • 33.
    For a uniformly random permutation of [10], find the probability that σ(1) ≠ 1 and σ(2) ≠ 2.
  • 34.
    How many 4-element subsets of [12] contain no two consecutive integers?
  • 35.
    Evaluate ∑ᵢ C(6, i)C(8, 5 − i).
  • 36.
    Use Lucas's theorem to find C(100, 50) mod 7.
  • 37.
    How many lattice paths with unit steps E and N go from (0, 0) to (6, 6) without ever rising above the diagonal?
  • 38.
    In how many ways can 10 doughnuts be chosen from 4 kinds if at most 3 may be of the first kind?
  • 39.
    Count the surjections from [7] onto [3] and deduce S(7, 3).
  • 40.
    In how many ways can 8 identical balls be placed into 3 identical boxes, empty boxes allowed?
  • 41.
    Unrank 100 in S₅ in lexicographic order (counting from 0).
  • 42.
    Find the 3-subset of ℕ = {0, 1, 2, …} whose colexicographic rank is 50, and find the colex rank of {2, 5, 6}.
  • 43.
    Which index i has binary reflected Gray code word 1101?
  • 44.
    Expand the Gaussian coefficient [5 2]q and use it to count the 2-dimensional subspaces of 𝔽₃⁵.
  • 45.
    How many colourings of the vertices of a square with 3 colours are there up to the full symmetry group D₄ of the square?

Challenge (Problems 46–60)

  • 46.
    Prove that Sₙ is generated by the transposition (1 2) and the n-cycle (1 2 ⋯ n).
  • 47.
    Prove that the centre of Sₙ is trivial for n ≥ 3.
  • 48.
    How many permutations σ ∈ S₈ satisfy σ³ = 1?
  • 49.
    Prove that for a uniformly random σ ∈ Sₙ the events Rᵢ = "position i is a record (left-to-right maximum)" are independent with P(Rᵢ) = 1/i, and deduce again that the expected number of records is Hₙ.
  • 50.
    In the hundred-prisoners problem with the cycle strategy, compute the success probability to four decimal places, and prove that for 2m prisoners opening m boxes each it tends to 1 − ln 2.
  • 51.
    Prove that the probability that 1 and 2 lie in the same cycle of a uniformly random permutation of [n] (n ≥ 2) is 1/2.
  • 52.
    How many permutations of [6] have exactly 3 inversions? What is the mean number of inversions of a random permutation of [6]?
  • 53.
    Prove the reciprocity law ((n k)) = (−1)ᵏ C(−n, k) and the identity ∑ₖ ((n k)) xᵏ = (1 − x)−n.
  • 54.
    Find the lexicographic rank (counting from 0) of 2 6 4 1 5 3 in S₆.
  • 55.
    Show that the number of k-subsets of [n] with no two elements cyclically adjacent (where n and 1 count as adjacent) is (n/(n − k))C(n − k, k) for 0 ≤ k < n, and evaluate it for n = 10, k = 3.
  • 56.
    How many 2-colour necklaces with 12 beads are there up to rotation?
  • 57.
    Use Burnside's lemma with S₄ acting on the 6 edges of the complete graph K₄ to count the isomorphism classes of simple graphs on 4 vertices.
  • 58.
    Find the exact probability that every cycle of a uniformly random permutation of [6] has length at most 3.
  • 59.
    Prove that for n ≥ 5 all 3-cycles are conjugate in Aₙ.
  • 60.
    Prove that the matrices of Stirling numbers [S(n, k)] and [s(n, k)] are mutually inverse, and verify ∑ₖ S(4, k)s(k, 2) = 0 explicitly.
