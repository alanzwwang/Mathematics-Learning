Problem Set

60 problems

Overview12345678910111213ProblemsAssessment

The problems are grouped by difficulty. The Core problems check fluency with the computations of each lesson; the Advanced problems require combining several ideas or writing a short proof; the Challenge problems are of the kind found on graduate qualifying examinations. Unless stated otherwise, polynomials have rational coefficients and fields have characteristic 0.

Core (Problems 1–20)

  • 1.
    Solve the system x + 2y + z = 4, 3x − y + 2z = 15, x + y − z = −3 by Gaussian elimination.
  • 2.
    For which k does the system x + 2y = 3, 2x + ky = 6 have a unique solution, infinitely many solutions, or none?
  • 3.
    Find the rank of the matrix with rows (1, 2, 3), (4, 5, 6), (7, 8, 9) and a basis of its kernel.
  • 4.
    Find the rank of the matrix with rows (1, 2, 1, 0), (2, 4, 0, 2), (3, 6, 1, 2) and a basis of the solution space of the homogeneous system.
  • 5.
    Divide x⁵ + 1 by x² + 1, giving quotient and remainder.
  • 6.
    Compute gcd(x⁴ − 1, x⁶ − 1) in ℚ[x], and state the general formula for gcd(xᵃ − 1, xᵇ − 1).
  • 7.
    Find all rational roots of 6x³ − 11x² + 6x − 1.
  • 8.
    Prove that x⁴ + 10x + 5 is irreducible over ℚ.
  • 9.
    Is x³ + 2x + 1 irreducible over 𝔽₃? Over ℚ?
  • 10.
    Let α, β, γ be the roots of x³ + 2x² − 5x + 1. Compute α² + β² + γ² and 1/α + 1/β + 1/γ.
  • 11.
    Find the monic cubic with roots 1, 2 and −4.
  • 12.
    Depress x³ − 6x² + 11x − 6 by a translation and solve it.
  • 13.
    Solve x³ − 6x − 9 = 0 by Cardano's formula, and find all three roots.
  • 14.
    Compute the discriminant of x³ − 7x + 6 and say what it tells you about the roots.
  • 15.
    Solve x² + x + 1 = 0 in 𝔽₁₃.
  • 16.
    Let α, β, γ be the roots of x³ − x − 1. Compute α³ + β³ + γ³.
  • 17.
    Find [ℚ(√2, √5) : ℚ] and the minimal polynomial of √2 + √5 over ℚ.
  • 18.
    Find the minimal polynomials over ℚ of 1 + √3 and of 1 + ∛2.
  • 19.
    Apply three steps of Newton's method to x³ − 2 starting from x₀ = 1, and compare with ∛2.
  • 20.
    Find all roots of x² − 1 in ℤ/8ℤ, and explain why this does not contradict Corollary 2.3.

Advanced (Problems 21–45)

  • 21.
    Discuss the system x + y + az = 1, x + ay + z = 1, ax + y + z = 1 for all real a, using rank.
  • 22.
    Let a ≠ b. Find the remainder of f ∈ K[x] on division by (x − a)(x − b) in terms of f(a) and f(b).
  • 23.
    Find gcd(f, f′) and the square-free part of f = x⁵ − 3x⁴ + 4x³ − 4x² + 3x − 1.
  • 24.
    Show that x³ + x + 1 is irreducible over 𝔽₂ and over ℚ, and describe the field 𝔽₂[x]/(x³ + x + 1).
  • 25.
    Factor x⁴ + 1 over 𝔽₂, 𝔽₃ and 𝔽₅, and prove that it is irreducible over ℚ.
  • 26.
    Prove that x⁴ − 4x³ + 6x² − 4x + 3 is irreducible over ℚ.
  • 27.
    Let f ∈ ℤ[x] be monic with f(0) and f(1) both odd. Prove that f has no rational root.
  • 28.
    Apply Cardano's formula to x³ − 7x + 6. Why must complex numbers appear, and why does this not contradict the casus irreducibilis theorem in a deeper sense?
  • 29.
    Solve x³ − 3x − 1 = 0 trigonometrically, giving the roots to four decimal places.
  • 30.
    Solve y⁴ − 6y² + 8y − 3 = 0 by Ferrari's method, and explain the multiplicity of the resolvent root.
  • 31.
    Compute the discriminant of y⁴ + y + 1 and decide how many real roots it has.
  • 32.
    Let t₁, t₂, t₃ be the roots of t³ + pt + q. Express (t₁ − t₂)² + (t₁ − t₃)² + (t₂ − t₃)² in terms of p and q.
  • 33.
    Let α, β, γ be the roots of x³ − x − 1. Compute α⁵ + β⁵ + γ⁵ in two ways.
  • 34.
    Express x₁³ + x₂³ + x₃³ − 3x₁x₂x₃ in terms of elementary symmetric polynomials.
  • 35.
    Compute Res(x² − 2, x³ − 3).
  • 36.
    Use a resultant to find the intersection points of x² + y² = 1 and y = x² − 1, and reconcile the count with Bézout's theorem.
  • 37.
    Compute Disc(x⁵ − 6x + 3) and check its sign against the number of real roots.
  • 38.
    Factor x⁴ + 4 into real quadratics, and explain why every real polynomial is a product of real factors of degree at most 2.
  • 39.
    Prove the Cauchy bound and apply it to 2x³ − 3x² − 3x + 2.
  • 40.
    Find the degree of the splitting field of x⁴ − 2 over ℚ and identify its Galois group.
  • 41.
    Show that Gal(ℚ(ζ₅)/ℚ) is cyclic of order 4 and find its unique quadratic subfield.
  • 42.
    Show that ℚ(√2 + i) = ℚ(√2, i) and find the minimal polynomial of √2 + i.
  • 43.
    Determine the Galois groups over ℚ of x³ − x − 1 and of x³ − 7x − 7.
  • 44.
    How many fields lie between ℚ and the splitting field of x³ − 2 (inclusive)? List them.
  • 45.
    Analyse Newton's method for f = (x − 1)²(x + 2) near x = 1, and give an iteration that converges quadratically there.

Challenge (Problems 46–60)

  • 46.
    Prove that the conjugacy classes of A₅ have sizes 1, 15, 20, 12, 12.
  • 47.
    Prove that x⁵ − 10x + 5 is not solvable by radicals over ℚ.
  • 48.
    Express cos(2π/5) and a primitive fifth root of unity in radicals, and explain why x⁵ − 1 is solvable.
  • 49.
    Prove that the roots of x³ − 3x + 1 cannot be expressed using only real radicals.
  • 50.
    Prove that the regular heptagon cannot be constructed with ruler and compass.
  • 51.
    How many intermediate fields does the splitting field of x⁴ − 2 over ℚ have?
  • 52.
    For Newton's method on x² − 2, compute lim (xₙ₊₁ − √2)/(xₙ − √2)², and prove the exact error identity.
  • 53.
    Show that Newton's method for x³ − 2x + 2 has a superattracting 2-cycle.
  • 54.
    Prove the fundamental theorem of algebra from Liouville's theorem.
  • 55.
    Using the product formula, prove Res(f, gh) = Res(f, g) Res(f, h) and Res(g, f) = (−1)mn Res(f, g).
  • 56.
    Count the monic irreducible cubics over 𝔽ₚ, and list them for p = 2.
  • 57.
    Prove that xᵖ − x − a is irreducible over 𝔽ₚ for every a ≠ 0 in 𝔽ₚ.
  • 58.
    Show that Gal(x⁴ + 1) over ℚ is the Klein four-group, and use this to explain why x⁴ + 1 is reducible modulo every prime.
  • 59.
    Use a Sturm sequence to count the roots of x⁴ − 5x² + 4 in (0, 3).
  • 60.
    Given that x⁴ + 8x + 12 is irreducible over ℚ, show that its Galois group is A₄.
