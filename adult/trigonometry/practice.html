Problem Set

60 problems

Overview123456789101112ProblemsAssessment

The problems are grouped by difficulty. Core problems check the definitions and standard computations of each lesson; Advanced problems require combining several results or writing a short proof; Challenge problems are at the level of a qualifying examination and some of them develop results that were only sketched in the lessons. Angles are in radians unless marked with a degree sign, and numerical answers should be given to the stated accuracy.

Core (Problems 1–20)

  • 1.
    Compute cos(1/2) to six decimal places from the power series, and justify the accuracy with an explicit error bound.
  • 2.
    Prove from the power series that (sin x)′ = cos x and (cos x)′ = −sin x, and deduce that both functions satisfy y″ = −y.
  • 3.
    Solve y″ + y = 0 with y(0) = 1 and y′(0) = √3, and write the solution in the form R cos(x − φ).
  • 4.
    Derive the formulas for cos 2θ and sin 2θ from Euler's formula, and deduce cos²θ = (1 + cos 2θ)/2.
  • 5.
    Linearise cos⁴θ and use the result to evaluate ∫₀2π cos⁴θ dθ.
  • 6.
    Find the exact values of sin 75°, cos 15° and tan 15°.
  • 7.
    Compute arcsin(sin 5) and arccos(cos 5) exactly.
  • 8.
    Prove by differentiation that arctan x + arctan(1/x) = π/2 for x > 0. What is the value for x < 0?
  • 9.
    Show that 2 arctan(1/3) + arctan(1/7) = π/4.
  • 10.
    Find all three angles (to two decimals, in degrees) of the triangle with sides 7, 8, 9.
  • 11.
    A triangle has a = 5, b = 8 and C = 60°. Find c and the area.
  • 12.
    In a triangle, a = 6, b = 8 and A = 30°. Find both possible values of c exactly.
  • 13.
    For the 5–12–13 right triangle, find R, r and the distance OI between the circumcentre and the incentre.
  • 14.
    Find the great-circle distance between the points (0° N, 0° E) and (45° N, 90° E) on a sphere of radius 6371 km.
  • 15.
    A spherical triangle has angles 80°, 70° and 60°. Find its area on the unit sphere and on a sphere of radius 6371 km.
  • 16.
    Show that c = cos(π/9) is a root of 8x³ − 6x − 1.
  • 17.
    Compute T₃(T₂(x)) and check that it equals T₆(x).
  • 18.
    Evaluate ∑ₖ₌₁¹⁰ sin(kπ/3) using the closed formula for ∑ sin kx, and check the result directly.
  • 19.
    Find the Fourier series of f(x) = |sin x| and deduce the value of ∑ₘ₌₁∞ 1/(4m² − 1).
  • 20.
    Solve sinh x = 3/4 and tanh x = 1/2.

Advanced (Problems 21–45)

  • 21.
    Show from the series that 5/6 < sin 1 < 101/120.
  • 22.
    Prove that the set of real periods of cos is exactly 2πℤ.
  • 23.
    Prove that sin 3θ = 4 sin θ sin(π/3 − θ) sin(π/3 + θ).
  • 24.
    Estimate π from Machin's formula using five terms of the series for arctan(1/5) and two terms of the series for arctan(1/239), and bound the error.
  • 25.
    Show that arctan(1/(2n²)) = arctan(n/(n + 1)) − arctan((n − 1)/n), and evaluate ∑ₙ₌₁∞ arctan(1/(2n²)).
  • 26.
    Find all complex solutions of cos z = 3.
  • 27.
    Show that |sin(x + iy)|² = sin²x + sinh²y, and deduce again that all zeros of sin in ℂ are real.
  • 28.
    Use the law of tangents to solve the triangle with a = 9, b = 5 and C = 60°.
  • 29.
    Prove Mollweide's formula (a + b)/c = cos((A − B)/2)/sin(C/2).
  • 30.
    Prove that in every triangle cot A cot B + cot B cot C + cot C cot A = 1.
  • 31.
    Prove r = 4R sin(A/2) sin(B/2) sin(C/2) and verify it for the 13–14–15 triangle.
  • 32.
    Prove Δ = 2R² sin A sin B sin C and verify it for the 13–14–15 triangle.
  • 33.
    A spherical right triangle (C = 90°) on the unit sphere has legs a = b = 45°. Find c, the angles A and B, and the area.
  • 34.
    Show that an equilateral spherical triangle with side a has angle A with cos A = cos a/(1 + cos a). For which a is A = 120°, and what configuration is this?
  • 35.
    Derive the haversine formula from the spherical law of cosines, and explain why it is preferred for short distances.
  • 36.
    Two points lie at latitude 60° N with longitudes 0° and 180°. Compare the great-circle distance with the distance along the parallel, in degrees of arc.
  • 37.
    Compute T₂(T₃(x)) and explain without calculation why it equals T₃(T₂(x)).
  • 38.
    Verify that T₅′ = 5U₄ by direct computation.
  • 39.
    Find the best uniform approximation of x³ on [0, 2] by polynomials of degree at most 2, and its error.
  • 40.
    Evaluate ∑ₖ₌₁⁵ csc²(kπ/6) directly and compare with Theorem 8.2.
  • 41.
    Evaluate cos(π/7) cos(2π/7) cos(3π/7) and cos(π/7) cos(2π/7) cos(4π/7).
  • 42.
    For a ∉ ℤ, find the Fourier series of cos(ax) on [−π, π] and deduce the partial fraction expansion of π cot πa.
  • 43.
    Use Parseval's identity for the odd 2π-periodic function equal to x(π − x) on [0, π] to evaluate the sum of 1/n⁶ over odd n.
  • 44.
    Solve 2 cosh x + sinh x = 2.
  • 45.
    Prove that gd(x) = 2 arctan(tanh(x/2)) and evaluate ∫₀∞ sech x dx.

Challenge (Problems 46–60)

  • 46.
    Determine all rational numbers r such that cos(rπ) is rational.
  • 47.
    Suppose θ is a rational multiple of π and both cos θ and sin θ are rational. Show that (cos θ, sin θ) is one of (±1, 0), (0, ±1).
  • 48.
    Find the minimal polynomials over ℚ of 2 cos(2π/5) and 2 cos(2π/9), and check their degrees against Theorem 12.1.
  • 49.
    Find the degree of cos(2π/n) over ℚ for n = 15, 17 and 360, and decide which of the regular 15-gon and 17-gon are constructible.
  • 50.
    Show that for 0 < δ < π the series ∑ sin nx/n converges uniformly on [δ, 2π − δ].
  • 51.
    For the sawtooth f(x) = x on (−π, π), show that the maximum of the partial sum Sₙf on (0, π) tends to 2 Si(π) ≈ 3.704.
  • 52.
    Derive Wallis's product from the product formula for sine, and show that the partial products Pₙ = ∏ₖ₌₁ⁿ 4k²/(4k² − 1) increase to π/2.
  • 53.
    Use Theorem 8.1 to prove ∫₀π ln sin x dx = −π ln 2, justifying the passage to the limit.
  • 54.
    Prove that ∑ₖ₌₀ⁿ⁻¹ csc²(x + kπ/n) = n² csc²(nx) and deduce ∑ₖ₌₁ⁿ⁻¹ csc²(kπ/n) = (n² − 1)/3.
  • 55.
    In the hyperbolic plane of curvature −1, find the side length of the equilateral triangle with angles π/4, and its area.
  • 56.
    Show that sin 1° is algebraic of degree 48 over ℚ.
  • 57.
    Find all twice-differentiable f: ℝ → ℝ with f(0) = 1 satisfying f(x + y) + f(x − y) = 2 f(x) f(y) for all x, y.
  • 58.
    Show that the Lebesgue constants Lₙ = (1/π) ∫₋ππ |Dₙ(t)| dt satisfy Lₙ ≥ (4/π²) ∑ₖ₌₁ⁿ 1/k, and explain why this implies that some continuous function has a Fourier series diverging at 0.
  • 59.
    Use the cotangent expansion to prove ∑ₙ₌₁∞ 1/(n² + 1) = (π coth π − 1)/2.
  • 60.
    Using the minimal polynomial of 2 cos(2π/7), evaluate sec(2π/7) + sec(4π/7) + sec(6π/7).
