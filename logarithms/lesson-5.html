⚡ The Landmarks — 0, 1, and the Questions You Can't Ask

📖 Lesson 5 of 8

👋 Start12345678🏋️ Practice🏆 Quiz

📌 Key ideas of this section

log of 1 is ALWAYS 0 (in every base).        log₂(1) = 0
log of the base is ALWAYS 1.                 log₅(5) = 1
log of 0 (or a negative) does not exist.

Landmark 1: log(1) = 0

What power of 2 gives you 1? Multiply zero 2s! An "empty multiplication" leaves just 1 — that's why mathematicians say 2⁰ = 1. So:

log₂(1) = 0        log₁₀(1) = 0        log₇(1) = 0

Every base, same answer. Remember Lesson 3's zero-counting: the number 1 has no zeros — and its log is 0. Two routes, same landmark.

Landmark 2: log of the base = 1

What power of 5 gives 5? Just one: 5¹ = 5. So log₅(5) = 1. Always:

log₂(2) = 1        log₁₀(10) = 1        log₃(3) = 1

The forbidden questions

Can you find log₂(0)? That asks: "what power of 2 equals 0?" Try some: 2¹ = 2, 2² = 4, 2³ = 8... multiplying 2s only makes things bigger — never 0. And 2⁰ = 1, not 0. There is NO answer, so log(0) does not exist. (Calculators say "Error." Now you know why!)

Negative targets are just as impossible: powers of 2 are always positive, so log₂(−8) can't be found either. You can only take logs of numbers bigger than 0.

The bonus ladder: going below zero

Here's a peek at next year's math. The powers of 2 form a ladder — and each step down divides by 2:

8   = 2³        log₂(8) = 3
4   = 2²        log₂(4) = 2
2   = 2¹        log₂(2) = 1
1   = 2⁰        log₂(1) = 0
½   = 2⁻¹       log₂(½) = −1
¼   = 2⁻²       log₂(¼) = −2

The ladder doesn't stop at 1 — it keeps going into fractions, and the logs keep counting: past 0 into the negatives. So log₂(½) = −1. Don't worry if this feels weird — it's a challenge-problem bonus. The ladder is all you need: each step down, divide by 2 and subtract 1 from the log.
