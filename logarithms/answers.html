  • 86.

    log(8) = log(2 × 2 × 2) = 0.3 + 0.3 + 0.3 = 0.9.
    (Very close to the true values 0.602 and 0.903 — not bad for tenths!)

  • 88.

  • 91.
    log₂(½) = −1 (one step down from 1: divide by 2)
    log₂(⅛) = −3 (three steps down: 1 → ½ → ¼ → ⅛)
    log(1/100) = −2 (two steps below 1 in base 10: 1 → 1/10 → 1/100)
  • 92.
    a) Inside first: log₂(16) = 4. Then log₂(4) = 2. So log₂(log₂(16)) = 2.
    b) Inside first: log₂(8) = 3. Then log₃(3) = 1. So the answer is 1.
  • 93.
    4³ = 64 and log₂(64) = 6. Rule check: 3 × log₂(4) = 3 × 2 = 6 ✅
    100² = 10,000 and log(10,000) = 4. Rule check: 2 × log(100) = 2 × 2 = 4 ✅
  • 94.
    Any three of these: log₂(64) = 6 (2⁶ = 64) · log₄(64) = 3 (4³ = 64)
    log₈(64) = 2 (8² = 64) · log₆₄(64) = 1 (the base itself!). All four work —
    64 is a power of many numbers because 64 = 2⁶, and 6 has lots of factors!
  • 95.
    2⁶ = 64 (not past 100) and 2⁷ = 128 (past it!) → 7 doublings.
    log₂(100) would say it exactly — it sits between 6 and 7!
  • 96.
    Hour by hour: 6, 12, 24, 48, 96. After 4 hours: 48 (not past 50).
    After 5 hours: 96 (past it!) → 5 hours ✅
  • 97.
    a) Two steps: 100 times stronger.
    b) Four steps: 10 × 10 × 10 × 10 = 10⁴ = 10,000 times stronger.
    The steps apart come from SUBTRACTING, while the strengths MULTIPLY —
    that's the whole log idea in the wild!
  • 98.
    a) Between 3 and 4, because 8 < 12 < 16.
    b) log₂(12) = 2 + log₂(3), because 12 = 4 × 3 and log₂(4) = 2.
    c) log₂(3) sits between 1 and 2 (because 2 < 3 < 4), so adding 2 puts the
    total between 3 and 4 — the same sandwich! ✅
  • 99.
    Sample answers: log₂(8) = 3, log₃(27) = 3, log₅(125) = 3, and the invisible
    base: log(1,000) = 3. Yours may differ — raise any base to the 3rd power,
    put it inside its own log, and the answer is 3!
  • 100.
    Sample doubling problem: "A magic beanstalk doubles its height every day.
    How many days until it's 32 times as tall?" → log₂(32) = 5 days ✅
    Sample zeros problem: "A savings account multiplies by 10 every year (wow!).
    How many years to turn 1 dollar into 100,000?" → count the zeros:
    log(100,000) = 5 years ✅
    Your problems may be completely different — if one counts doublings and the
    other counts zeros, you're right.
