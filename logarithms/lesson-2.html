🧩 The Two Languages — Translating Logs and Powers

📖 Lesson 2 of 8

👋 Start12345678🏋️ Practice🏆 Quiz

📌 Key idea of this section

Every log sentence is a power sentence in disguise.
log₂(8) = 3   ⇄   2³ = 8.   Learn to translate both ways!

In Lesson 1 you met the big idea. Now let's make it automatic. Every log sentence has three parts, and each part has a name and a job:

log₂(8) = 3
     ↑   ↑   ↑
  base target power
  • ·
    The BASE is the number being multiplied (the little one, sitting low).
  • ·
    The TARGET is the number you're trying to build.
  • ·
    The POWER is the answer: how many times to multiply the base.

To translate from log language to power language, use this chant:

"BASE to the POWER equals TARGET."
log₂(8) = 3     →     "2 to the 3 equals 8"     →     2³ = 8 ✅
log₅(25) = 2    →     "5 to the 2 equals 25"    →     5² = 25 ✅

To translate back — from a power sentence to a log sentence — ask where each piece goes. The base stays the base. The power becomes the answer. The target goes inside the parentheses:

3⁴ = 81     →     log₃(81) = 4 ✅
2⁶ = 64     →     log₂(64) = 6 ✅

Why bother translating? Because some questions are easy in one language and hard in the other. "What is 2⁵?" — easy in power language (32). "What power of 2 gives 32?" — easy in log language (log₂(32) = 5). A good mathematician switches languages without thinking, like a bilingual kid switching between English and Spanish.

One more piece of vocabulary: the power is also called the EXPONENT. So "what power?" and "what exponent?" are the same question. In fact, some people define it that way: a logarithm IS an exponent!

✏️ Your Turn!

Translate into power language: a) log₃(27) = 3. b) log₇(49) = 2.

Translate into log language: c) 2⁵ = 32. d) 10² = 100.

👀 Show the answers

Answers: a) 3³ = 27. b) 7² = 49. c) log₂(32) = 5. d) log₁₀(100) = 2 — and Lesson 3 shows why we usually write that one WITHOUT the little 10!
